Horse Riding: Ride Slowly and Safely

Before you begin

Warm-Up Questions

  1. Have you ever seen a horse?

  2. Do you think horse riding looks fun or scary?

  3. What safety rule do you know for a sport?

Reading

1. What Is Horse Riding?

Horse riding is not only sitting on a horse. A rider learns how to meet the horse, sit well, hold the reins, and listen to the teacher. The horse and rider work together. At first, many riders only walk with the horse. Walking is slow and useful. The rider learns balance. The horse learns that the rider is calm.

Check your understanding

1. What do a horse and rider do together?

Answer

They work together.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: I would like to start with a slow walk. I would like to try a faster ride later. I want to watch first. Horse riding means...

Think one step further

  • Would you like to start with a slow walk or a faster ride?

Reading

2. Meet the Horse Slowly

Horses are big and strong, but many horses are gentle. A horse can still feel scared. A fast hand, a loud voice, or running feet can surprise a horse. Before riding, people should move slowly. They can say hello in a quiet voice. They can stand where the teacher says. They should not run behind the horse. This is the first safety lesson. A calm rider helps the horse feel calm too.

Check your understanding

1. Why should people move slowly near a horse?

Answer

Because a horse can feel scared if people move too fast.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: I can move slowly. I can use a quiet voice. I can listen to the teacher. I want to go slowly. The horse is calm.

Think one step further

  • What can you do to help an animal feel safe?

Reading

3. Saddle, Reins, and Simple Moves

A rider sits on a saddle. The saddle helps the rider sit in the right place. The rider holds the reins with both hands. The reins help the rider ask the horse to stop, turn left, or turn right. Good riders do not pull hard. They use small signals. They also use their body and legs. In a beginner lesson, the teacher may say, "Walk," "Stop," "Turn left," or "Turn right."

Check your understanding

1. What do the reins help the rider do?

Answer

The reins help the rider ask the horse where to go.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: Walk sounds easiest. Stop sounds important. Turn left sounds useful. Can we stop? Can we turn left? Can we turn right?

Think one step further

  • Which move sounds easiest: walk, stop, turn left, or turn right?

Reading

4. Safety Comes First

Many riders wear a helmet. A helmet protects the head. Riders also wear shoes or boots that are good for riding. They listen to the teacher before they get on the horse. In a lesson, the teacher checks the horse, the saddle, and the rider. The teacher can help the rider get on and get off. If the rider feels worried, the rider can say, "Can we go slowly?"

Check your understanding

1. Why do many riders wear a helmet?

Answer

A helmet protects the head.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: Wear a helmet. Listen to the teacher. Go slowly at first. Please help me.

Think one step further

  • What safety rule feels most important to you?

Reading

5. Jockeys and Famous Riders

A jockey is a person who rides a horse in a race. Jockeys are light, strong, and skilled. They practice balance, timing, and calm thinking. A good jockey does not only go fast. A horse can feel small signals from the jockey's hands, legs, and body. One famous Japanese jockey is Yutaka Take. People know him for many big wins and calm riding. A famous jockey trains a lot and learns how each horse moves. Many people ride horses only for fun, health, or time with animals.

Check your understanding

1. What does a jockey do?

Answer

A jockey rides a horse in a race.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: They can win important events. They can practice for many years. They can have a special style. People can remember their best moment.

Think one step further

  • Why can a sportsperson become famous?

Reading

Speaking Practice: Explain the First Ride

Practice after you understand the horse and the safety rules. Use the patterns above. Explain a first riding lesson in five small steps. First, meet the horse slowly. Next, put on a helmet. Then sit on the saddle. After that, hold the reins. Finally, walk slowly with the teacher.

Check your understanding

1. What is the first step in this practice?

Answer

The first step is to meet the horse slowly.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: I feel a little nervous. Can we go slowly? First, we walk.

Think one step further

  • Explain your first horse-riding lesson. Then change one detail: the feeling, the horse's size, or the first move.

Speak

Final Speaking Task

  1. What is horse riding?

  2. Why should people move slowly near a horse?

  3. What does a rider sit on?

  4. What do riders hold with their hands?

  5. Why do riders wear a helmet?

  6. What does a jockey do?

  7. How can a jockey become famous?

  8. Do you want to ride a horse? Why?

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Horse riding is a sport and a relationship with an animal. A good rider moves slowly, listens carefully, and uses small signals. Riding can be exciting, but safety comes first.
