Reading

1. What Is Horse Riding?

Horse riding is not only sitting on a horse.

A rider learns how to meet the horse, sit well, hold the reins, and listen to the teacher.

The horse and rider work together.

At first, many riders only walk with the horse.

Walking is slow and useful.

The rider learns balance.

The horse learns that the rider is calm.

Reading

2. Meet the Horse Slowly

Horses are big and strong, but many horses are gentle.

A horse can still feel scared.

A fast hand, a loud voice, or running feet can surprise a horse.

Before riding, people should move slowly.

They can say hello in a quiet voice.

They can stand where the teacher says.

They should not run behind the horse.

This is the first safety lesson.

A calm rider helps the horse feel calm too.

Reading

3. Saddle, Reins, and Simple Moves

A rider sits on a saddle.

The saddle helps the rider sit in the right place.

The rider holds the reins with both hands.

The reins help the rider ask the horse to stop, turn left, or turn right.

Good riders do not pull hard.

They use small signals.

They also use their body and legs.

In a beginner lesson, the teacher may say, "Walk," "Stop," "Turn left," or "Turn right."

Reading

4. Safety Comes First

Many riders wear a helmet.

A helmet protects the head.

Riders also wear shoes or boots that are good for riding.

They listen to the teacher before they get on the horse.

In a lesson, the teacher checks the horse, the saddle, and the rider.

The teacher can help the rider get on and get off.

If the rider feels worried, the rider can say, "Can we go slowly?"

Reading

5. Jockeys and Famous Riders

A jockey is a person who rides a horse in a race.

Jockeys are light, strong, and skilled.

They practice balance, timing, and calm thinking.

A good jockey does not only go fast.

A horse can feel small signals from the jockey's hands, legs, and body.

One famous Japanese jockey is Yutaka Take.

People know him for many big wins and calm riding.

A famous jockey trains a lot and learns how each horse moves.

Many people ride horses only for fun, health, or time with animals.
