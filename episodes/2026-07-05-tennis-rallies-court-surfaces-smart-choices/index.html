Reading

1. What a Rally Is

A tennis point starts with a serve. The other player tries to return the ball.

If both players keep hitting the ball over the net and inside the court, they are in a rally. A rally is like a small conversation with the ball.

One player hits deep to push the other player back. The next player may return safely to stay in the point. Then someone may see open space and attack.

2. Safe Shots and Risky Shots

A safe shot gives the player more room for error. It often goes higher over the net or deeper into the court.

Safe does not mean weak. A deep safe shot can make the opponent move back and wait.

A risky shot tries to finish the point faster. It may go close to a line, close to the net, or into a small open space.

If it works, the player may win the point. If it misses, the point is over.

Good tennis players do not attack every ball. They ask a quick question: Is this a good moment to stay safe, or is this the moment to take a risk?

3. Surfaces Change the Match

Tennis is played on different court surfaces. The main three are clay, grass, and hard court.

The surface changes how the ball bounces and how fast a point can feel. Clay often makes the ball slower and higher after the bounce.

This can make rallies longer, so patience matters. Grass often makes the ball stay lower and move faster, so quick reactions matter.

Hard courts are often more balanced, so many styles can work. The same player may need a different plan on each surface.

On clay, one more patient shot may be smart. On grass, a fast first move may be important. On a hard court, balance and timing can matter a lot.

4. Four Tournament Examples

The four Grand Slam tournaments show different tennis tests. The Australian Open is on hard courts in Melbourne.

Roland-Garros, also called the French Open, is on clay in Paris. Wimbledon is on grass in London.

The US Open is on hard courts in New York. These names are not only sports trivia.

They show why a player needs more than one plan. On clay, patience can help.

On grass, quick reactions can help. On hard courts, timing and balance can help.

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Tennis is simple to start: serve, return, and keep the ball in. But inside that simple rule, players solve many small problems. A rally needs patience. A safe shot can build pressure. A risky shot can finish the point. A court surface can change the whole plan.

When you watch tennis, try not to watch only the ball. Watch the choice. Did the player stay safe, attack open space, or change the plan because of the court?
