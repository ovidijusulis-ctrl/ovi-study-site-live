A2: Coffee Knowledge 1: Espresso Foundations

Quality checked lesson

Most coffee drinks in an Australian café start with espresso. You will learn what espresso is, how cafés build drinks from it, and why every new barista must learn the café's own recipe.

Introduction

Most in an café start with espresso. You will what espresso is, how cafés drinks from it, and why every new barista must learn the café's own recipe.

Before you begin

Warm-Up Questions

  1. Which coffee drinks do you know?

  2. Have you watched a barista make espresso?

  3. Why can the same drink taste different in two cafés?

Reading

1. Espresso Is a Brewing Method

Espresso is not one special bean. It is a way to make coffee. A machine pushes hot water through finely ground coffee under pressure. The result is a small, strong drink called an espresso shot. A café can use different beans and roast levels for espresso. The word espresso tells us about the brewing method, not the country, bean, or roast. This is an important difference. A dark-roasted bean is not automatically espresso, and a lighter-roasted bean can also be used in an espresso machine.

Check your understanding

1. What does the word espresso describe?

Answer

It describes a brewing method that uses hot water, fine coffee grounds, and pressure.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

  • What did you think espresso meant before this lesson?

Reading

2. One Base, Many Drinks

The espresso shot is the base of many Australian café drinks. A short black is espresso by itself. A long black adds hot water. A flat white, latte, and cappuccino add textured milk in different amounts and styles. A mocha adds chocolate and milk. Some cafés also serve espresso over ice or use it in cold milk drinks. The order may sound complicated, but the barista can listen for the drink base and the added parts. For example, a mocha needs espresso, chocolate, and milk. A long black needs espresso and water.

Check your understanding

1. What three parts are used in a mocha?

Answer

A mocha uses espresso, chocolate, and milk.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: I want to understand __. It is made with espresso and __.

Think one step further

  • Which espresso drink would you like to understand better?

Reading

3. A Barista Does More Than Press a Button

Making espresso is only one part of a barista's work. A barista grinds coffee, checks the grinder, prepares the coffee in the basket, starts the shot, and tastes the result. The barista also textures milk, cleans the machine, keeps the work area safe, and manages customer orders. Coffee changes during the day. Heat, humidity, and the age of the beans can change how quickly a shot runs. For this reason, a barista watches the coffee and adjusts the grinder when needed. Good training teaches the worker how to notice a problem and make one careful change.

Check your understanding

1. Why may a barista adjust the grinder during the day?

Answer

Coffee and café conditions can change, so the shot may start running differently.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: I think __ needs the most practice. A barista needs to notice __.

Think one step further

  • Which barista skill do you think needs the most practice?

Reading

4. Learn the House Recipe

Every café has a house recipe. The recipe tells the barista how much ground coffee to use, how much espresso to make, and how long the shot should take. It may also tell the worker how many shots go into each cup size. One café may use 18 grams of ground coffee. Another may use 20 or 22 grams. A large flat white may have two shots in one café and a different recipe in another. A new worker should not guess. The professional action is to ask, watch, measure, and follow the café's system.

Check your understanding

1. Why should a new barista learn the house recipe?

Answer

Coffee amounts, shot numbers, cup sizes, and drink methods can be different in each café.

Look back at this section and find the main reason.

Helpful clue: I would check how much coffee to use. How many shots go in this cup? Could you show me your house recipe?

Think one step further

  • What recipe detail would you check first on your first day?

Reading

Useful Speaking Patterns

- How much ground coffee do you use here? - How many shots go into this cup size? - Could you show me one before I try?

Story

It is Mia's first morning in an Australian café. She asks the head barista about the espresso recipe before making a flat white.

Roleplay

Dialogue Practice Role-play

Read once.

Mia

Good morning. I am ready to learn your espresso recipe.

Head Barista

Welcome, Mia. Let's start with the dose.

Mia

Thanks. How much ground coffee do you use here?

Head Barista

We use 20 grams and aim for 40 grams of espresso.

Mia

And how many shots go into a medium flat white?

Head Barista

We use two shots for that size.

Mia

Great. Could you show me one shot before I try?

Head Barista

Of course. Watch this one, and then you can make the next.

Mia

Thanks. I want to follow the house recipe correctly.

Head Barista

Good. Asking first is exactly the right thing to do.

Mia

Thank you. I am ready to start.

Head Barista

We will start together. See you there.

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Espresso is a brewing method and the base of many café drinks. A careful barista learns the whole process and follows the café's recipe. What is one question you would ask before making your first espresso at a new café?
