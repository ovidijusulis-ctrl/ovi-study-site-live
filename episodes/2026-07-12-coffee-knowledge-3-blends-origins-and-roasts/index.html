Before you begin

Warm-Up Questions

  1. What information do you notice on a coffee bag?

  2. Do you like chocolate, nut, fruit, or flower flavours?

  3. What makes a coffee easy to drink with milk?

Reading

1. What Is a Blend?

A blend combines two or more coffees. The coffees may come from different farms, regions, countries, plant varieties, or processing methods.

A roaster chooses the parts carefully to make one planned flavour. Blends can help a café offer a stable house coffee through the year.

If one seasonal coffee changes, the roaster can adjust another part of the blend. Many espresso blends are also designed to keep a clear coffee taste when milk is added. They may have familiar chocolate, caramel, or nut flavours that work well in a flat white or latte.

1. Why do cafés often use blends for milk drinks?

Sample answer

Roasters can design blends to keep a clear, balanced coffee flavour when milk is added.

Think one step further

What flavours would you choose for a house blend?

Helpful clue

  • I would choose __ and __ flavours.
  • These flavours may work well with milk.

Reading

2. What Is Single Origin?

Single origin means the coffee comes from one defined origin. The origin can be a country, a region, a cooperative, one farm, or even one small lot.

The label should tell the customer how specific the origin is. Cafés often serve single-origin coffee when they want people to notice a special flavour.

One coffee may remind people of berries or citrus. Another may have chocolate, nuts, or flowers.

These words describe flavour memories. They do not usually mean that berries, chocolate, or flowers were added to the beans.

1. Does single origin always mean one farm?

Sample answer

No. It can mean one country, region, cooperative, farm, or individual lot.

Think one step further

Which flavour note would make you curious about a coffee?

Helpful clue

  • I would like to try a coffee with __ notes.
  • It sounds __.

Reading

3. Light and Dark Roasts

Roasting changes green coffee into brown, aromatic coffee. A light roast usually keeps more of the coffee's original fruit, flower, and bright character.

It may also feel lighter in the mouth. A dark roast develops more roasted flavours.

It may taste more bitter, smoky, or like dark chocolate. Light does not always mean weak, and dark does not always mean stronger caffeine.

Roast level mainly tells us how far the roaster developed the beans. The final strength in the cup also depends on the dose, water, recipe, and brewing method.

Check your understanding

1. What flavours are more common in a dark roast?

Sample answer

Dark roasts more often have roasted, bitter, smoky, or dark-chocolate flavours.

Think one step further

Would you choose a light or dark roast for your next coffee? Why?

Helpful clue

  • I would choose a __ roast because __.
  • I prefer __ flavours.

Reading

4. Acidity Is Not Always Sourness

Coffee professionals often use the word acidity for a bright, lively taste. Pleasant acidity can remind people of orange, apple, berries, or sparkling fruit.

It can make a coffee feel fresh and interesting. Bad sourness is different.

It can taste sharp, aggressive, and unbalanced. A poorly made espresso may taste sour because the water did not take enough flavour from the coffee.

However, a fruity light roast can be well made and still have pleasant acidity. A good barista listens carefully before deciding that a customer dislikes all acidity.

1. What is the difference between pleasant acidity and unpleasant sourness?

Sample answer

Pleasant acidity is bright and balanced, while unpleasant sourness is sharp and unbalanced.

Think one step further

How could you explain acidity without making it sound negative?

Helpful clue

  • It has a bright taste like __.
  • It is fruity and balanced, not sharply sour.
  • Would you like something rounder and more chocolate-like?

Roleplay

Dialogue Practice Role-play

Practise with the dialogue audio

Audio unavailable. Please try again.

Read once.

Customer

I usually drink dark-roasted coffee. What is your house coffee like?

Barista

Our house blend has chocolate and caramel flavours. It works well with milk.

Customer

And what is the single origin like?

Barista

It is lighter and fruitier, with a bright orange-like acidity.

Customer

Is it very sour?

Barista

No. It is bright but balanced. The house blend is rounder and less fruity.

Customer

I think I will try the house blend as a flat white today.

Barista

Great choice. I will make that for you now.

Customer

Thanks for explaining it clearly.

Barista

You're welcome. I will call your name when it is ready.

Customer

Great. Thank you.

Barista

Have a good day.

Speak

Final Speaking Task

  1. Explain why a café may use a blend and what single origin means.

  2. Compare a light roast and a dark roast.

  3. Recommend one coffee to a customer who wants milk and a strong coffee flavour.

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Coffee labels are useful when they help a person imagine the cup. A good barista gives a short, honest explanation and checks what the customer enjoys. How would you describe your ideal coffee in three simple phrases?
