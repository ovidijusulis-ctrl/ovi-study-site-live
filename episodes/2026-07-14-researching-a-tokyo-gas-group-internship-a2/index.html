Reading

Parent Company and Subsidiary

When one company owns another company, we use two words. The big one is the parent company.

The smaller one is a subsidiary. In Japanese, people say oyagaisha for the parent and kogaisha for the subsidiary.

A subsidiary is not just a branch office. It is a real company with its own name, its own offices, and its own staff.

The parent company owns shares of the subsidiary, so it has power over big decisions. But the daily work happens inside the subsidiary.

What a Group Company Means

Japanese business people also use the word 'group company' or gurūpu-gaisha. This means a company in the same family of businesses.

So when your son gets an offer from Tokyo Gas Yamanashi, this is important. It is a group company of Tokyo Gas.

It is not the same office as Tokyo Gas in the capital. It has its own team in Yamanashi Prefecture.

Reading

The Main Tokyo Gas Group

Tokyo Gas Co., Ltd. is the main company. It was founded in 1885, more than 130 years ago.

It is one of the largest city gas companies in Japan. It serves the Tokyo area, including Tokyo, Kanagawa, Chiba, Saitama, and some other places nearby.

Millions of homes and many factories use its city gas. Inside the Tokyo Gas group, there is also a company called Tokyo Gas Network. This company manages the pipelines under the streets.

Reading

Tokyo Gas Yamanashi

Tokyo Gas Yamanashi is the regional group company for Yamanashi Prefecture. It is owned by Tokyo Gas, so Tokyo Gas is the parent company.

But Tokyo Gas Yamanashi has its own office, its own workers, and its own customers in Yamanashi. It handles two kinds of gas.

First, city gas: gas that comes to homes through pipes in the towns. Second, LPG, also called propane gas.

Reading

Regional Gas Work

LPG is sold in tanks and is common in mountain areas and small villages where there are no gas pipes. Yamanashi has many small towns and mountain areas, so both kinds of gas are needed.

This company is much smaller than the main Tokyo Gas in the capital, but the work is still real and important. If your son works here as an intern, he will see gas service in a countryside area, not in a big city.

Reading

Check the Exact Company

Here are simple steps to research the internship. First, take the offer letter and write down the exact legal company name.

It is important to copy it exactly, because 'Tokyo Gas' and 'Tokyo Gas Yamanashi' are not the same company. Second, search online for that exact company's official website.

Be careful: some search results are old articles or copies. Look for the real, official site.

Third, open the company overview page. In Japanese this is called kaisha gaiyō.

Reading

Check the Internship Itself

This page shows the address, the number of workers, the year the company started, and the main business. Fourth, check who the parent company is.

The overview page usually says this clearly. Fifth, read what the internship program covers: how many days, what tasks, and what the intern will learn.

Also prepare questions to ask: which department will he join, what does a normal day look like, and who will be his mentor. In the end, a famous name matters less than what your son will really see, learn, and practice every day.

Tokyo Gas Yamanashi belongs to the Tokyo Gas group, but it is its own regional company. A careful decision starts with the exact legal name, official company overview, and internship details.
