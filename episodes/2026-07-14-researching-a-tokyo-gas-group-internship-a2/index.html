A2: Researching Your Son's Internship: Tokyo Gas Yamanashi

Quality checked lesson

Learn how Tokyo Gas, Tokyo Gas Network, and Tokyo Gas Yamanashi are related. Then practise checking an internship offer and asking about the department, daily work, and mentor.

Introduction

You will learn how Tokyo Gas Yamanashi is connected to the main Tokyo Gas group and how to research an internship offer. You will also prepare clear, polite questions about the department, daily , mentor, and parent .

Before you begin

Warm-Up

  1. When your child gets a job or internship offer, what do you usually want to know first?

  2. Do you think a famous company name always means a good workplace?

Reading

Parent Company and Subsidiary

When one company owns another company, we use two words. The big one is the parent company. The smaller one is a subsidiary. In Japanese, people say oyagaisha for the parent and kogaisha for the subsidiary. A subsidiary is not just a branch office. It is a real company with its own name, its own offices, and its own staff. The parent company owns shares of the subsidiary, so it has power over big decisions. But the daily work happens inside the subsidiary.

Check your understanding

1. What does a subsidiary keep, even when a parent company owns it?

Answer

Its own name, offices, and staff

A subsidiary is a real company, even though a parent company owns it.

Helpful clue: A subsidiary has its own __. A branch is part of __.

Think one step further

  • What is one difference between a subsidiary and a branch office?

Reading

What a Group Company Means

Japanese business people also use the word 'group company' or gurūpu-gaisha. This means a company in the same family of businesses. So when your son gets an offer from Tokyo Gas Yamanashi, this is important. It is a group company of Tokyo Gas. It is not the same office as Tokyo Gas in the capital. It has its own team in Yamanashi Prefecture.

Check your understanding

1. What does “group company” mean here?

Answer

A company in the same family of businesses

Tokyo Gas Yamanashi is in the Tokyo Gas business group, but it has its own regional team.

Helpful clue: It is useful because the office may be __. The work may be __.

Think one step further

  • Why is it useful to know the exact group company?

Reading

The Main Tokyo Gas Group

Tokyo Gas Co., Ltd. is the main company. It was founded in 1885, more than 130 years ago. It is one of the largest city gas companies in Japan. It serves the Tokyo area, including Tokyo, Kanagawa, Chiba, Saitama, and some other places nearby. Millions of homes and many factories use its city gas. Inside the Tokyo Gas group, there is also a company called Tokyo Gas Network. This company manages the pipelines under the streets.

Check your understanding

1. When was Tokyo Gas Co., Ltd. founded?

Answer

1885

The main Tokyo Gas company was founded in 1885.

Helpful clue: I would want to know __. I would check __ first.

Think one step further

  • What information would you want about a very large company group?

Reading

Why the Internship Company Is Different

In Japan, the law now separates the gas pipes from the gas sales. So one company sells the gas, and another company takes care of the pipes. This is the parent group. It is very big and well known. But remember: the offer for your son is not from this main company. It is from a smaller group company in Yamanashi.

Check your understanding

1. Is the internship offer from the main Tokyo Gas company in the capital?

Answer

No, it is from a smaller group company in Yamanashi

The famous group name is connected, but the legal company and workplace are different.

Helpful clue: The offer is from __. The workplace may be __.

Think one step further

  • Why should you not assume the internship is in central Tokyo?

Reading

Tokyo Gas Yamanashi

Tokyo Gas Yamanashi is the regional group company for Yamanashi Prefecture. It is owned by Tokyo Gas, so Tokyo Gas is the parent company. But Tokyo Gas Yamanashi has its own office, its own workers, and its own customers in Yamanashi. It handles two kinds of gas. First, city gas: gas that comes to homes through pipes in the towns. Second, LPG, also called propane gas.

Check your understanding

1. What two kinds of gas does Tokyo Gas Yamanashi handle?

Answer

City gas and LPG propane

The company handles piped city gas and LPG sold in tanks.

Helpful clue: I would expect __ because __. In a mountain town, __ may be easier.

Think one step further

  • Which gas service would you expect in a mountain town: city gas or LPG? Why?

Reading

Regional Gas Work

LPG is sold in tanks and is common in mountain areas and small villages where there are no gas pipes. Yamanashi has many small towns and mountain areas, so both kinds of gas are needed. This company is much smaller than the main Tokyo Gas in the capital, but the work is still real and important. If your son works here as an intern, he will see gas service in a countryside area, not in a big city.

Check your understanding

1. What kind of area would the intern learn about?

Answer

Gas service in countryside and mountain areas

Yamanashi needs both city gas and LPG because it has towns, villages, and mountain areas.

Helpful clue: An intern could learn about __. Regional work is useful because __.

Think one step further

  • What could an intern learn from working in a regional company?

Reading

Check the Exact Company

Here are simple steps to research the internship. First, take the offer letter and write down the exact legal company name. It is important to copy it exactly, because 'Tokyo Gas' and 'Tokyo Gas Yamanashi' are not the same company. Second, search online for that exact company's official website. Be careful: some search results are old articles or copies. Look for the real, official site. Third, open the company overview page. In Japanese this is called kaisha gaiyō.

Check your understanding

1. Which page gives basic facts such as the address, staff size, and main business?

Answer

The company overview page

The official company overview, or kaisha gaiyō, is the basic fact-checking page.

Helpful clue: I would check __ first because __. The most useful detail is __.

Think one step further

  • What detail on the company overview page would you check first?

Reading

Check the Internship Itself

This page shows the address, the number of workers, the year the company started, and the main business. Fourth, check who the parent company is. The overview page usually says this clearly. Fifth, read what the internship program covers: how many days, what tasks, and what the intern will learn. Also prepare questions to ask: which department will he join, what does a normal day look like, and who will be his mentor. In the end, a famous name matters less than what your son will really see, learn, and practice every day.

Check your understanding

1. What should you check in the internship program details?

Answer

The number of days, tasks, and what the intern will learn

The real learning experience matters more than a famous group name.

Helpful clue: The most important question is __. I need to know __ because __.

Think one step further

  • Which internship question is most important to ask before accepting the offer?

Speak

Prepare for a Real Conversation

These are preparation lines, not a roleplay. Read them aloud before you contact the company. During the real conversation, write short notes about the department, daily tasks, mentor, company relationship, and any safety information.

  1. Hello, my name is ___. My son received an internship offer from your company, and I would like to ask a few questions.

  2. Could you tell me which department he will join?

  3. What does a normal day look like for the intern?

  4. Who will be his mentor?

  5. May I confirm that Tokyo Gas is the parent company?

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Tokyo Gas Yamanashi belongs to the Tokyo Gas group, but it is its own regional company. A careful decision starts with the exact legal name, official company overview, and internship details. Before your conversation, prepare questions about the department, daily work, mentor, and parent company—and write down the answers.
