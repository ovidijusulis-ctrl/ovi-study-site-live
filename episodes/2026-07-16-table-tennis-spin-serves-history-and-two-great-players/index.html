Reading

Spin Changes the Ball

Players brush the ball with the racket to make spin.

The three basic kinds are topspin, backspin, and sidespin.

Topspin makes the ball dip and move forward quickly after the bounce.

Backspin can make it stay low and slow.

Sidespin can make it curve left or right.

Spin is often hard to see before the bounce.

Its effect is easier to see after the ball hits the table.

A player watches the bounce and changes the next shot.

Reading

Every Point Starts with a Serve

Every point starts with a serve.

In an official serve, the ball begins on an open palm.

The server tosses it nearly straight up at least 16 centimetres.

The server hits the ball as it falls.

The ball first bounces on the server’s side of the table.

Then it bounces on the other side.

A server can change the speed, place, and spin.

These changes can make the next shot difficult.

A short backspin serve and a fast topspin serve can start two very different points.

Reading

From a Dining Table to a World Sport

Table tennis began in England in the 1880s.

People played it as an indoor form of lawn tennis.

They used a dining table and simple equipment they had around them.

The sport later became more organised.

The International Table Tennis Federation, or ITTF, began in 1926.

The first World Championships also took place in 1926, in London.

A game for indoor tables had become an international sport.

Today, the table is still small, but players compete around the world.

Reading

Two Olympic Stars

Ma Long represents China.

He won the Olympic men’s singles title in 2016 and again at Tokyo 2020.

He became the first man to win this Olympic singles title twice.

Mima Ito represents Japan.

At Tokyo 2020, she and Jun Mizutani won the first Olympic mixed doubles table tennis title.

It was also Japan’s first Olympic gold medal in table tennis.

Ma Long’s result shows success across two Olympic Games.

Mima Ito’s result marks two important firsts in one event.
