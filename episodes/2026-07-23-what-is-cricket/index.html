Introduction

Cricket is a sport played by two teams of eleven players. One team bats, and the other team bowls and fields.

The game uses a bat, ball, pitch, and wickets. Teams score runs and play in three main match formats. This explainer has at least five short parts with an immediate guided check after each part, so you can learn one idea at a time.

  2. How many players do you think are on one cricket team?

  3. Look at the word wicket. What do you think a wicket might be in a sport?

    Need a starting line?

    This word is very important in cricket. You will learn more about it soon.

Reading

Part 1: The Teams and the Aim

Cricket is played by two teams. Each team has eleven players.

One team bats. The other team fields and bowls.

The batting team tries to score as many runs as possible. The fielding team tries to get the batters out.

An innings can end when ten batters are out or when the team finishes its overs. Then the other team bats. The team with more runs usually wins.

Check your understanding

Check: The Teams and the Aim

1. What does the batting team try to do, and what does the fielding team try to do?

Sample answer

These are the two opposite goals of the game.

Think one step further

If you played cricket, would you rather bat or field? Why?

Reading

Part 2: The Pitch and the Wickets

The pitch is a flat strip of ground in the middle of the field. It is 22 yards long.

At each end of the pitch there is a wicket. A wicket is made of three wooden sticks called stumps.

Two small pieces of wood called bails sit on top of the stumps. The bowler stands at one end and sends the ball toward the wicket at the other end. The batter stands in front of their wicket to protect it.

Check: The Pitch and the Wickets

1. What is a wicket made of, and why does the batter stand in front of it?

Sample answer

The wicket is the target. Protecting it is the batter's main job.

Think one step further

Why do you think the bails are important? What might happen if they fall off?

Reading

Part 3: Batting and Scoring Runs

The batter uses a flat bat to hit the ball. When the ball is hit, the two batters at the wickets can run to each other’s end.

Each completed run adds one run to the score. If the ball reaches the boundary rope around the field, the batting team gets four runs without running. If the ball flies over the boundary without bouncing, the team gets six runs.

Sample answer

A six is the biggest single score from one ball in cricket.

Think one step further

Reading

Part 4: Bowling and Overs

The bowler runs up and bowls the ball toward the batter. The ball may bounce once on the pitch before it reaches the batter.

Six valid balls from one bowler make one over. After each over, a different bowler takes the ball from the other end. One bowler cannot bowl two overs in a row.

Check: Bowling and Overs

1. How many balls are in one over, and can the same bowler bowl two overs in a row?

Sample answer

This rule keeps the game fair and means teams need several good bowlers.

Think one step further

Why do you think cricket uses the word over for a set of six balls?

Reading

Part 5: Common Ways to Get Out

There are several ways a batter can be dismissed. Bowled means the ball hits the stumps and the bails fall off.

Caught means a fielder catches a ball hit by the bat before it touches the ground. Run out means the batter is outside the crease line when a fielder breaks the wicket with the ball during a run. Ten dismissals can end an innings.

Check: Common Ways to Get Out

1. What is the difference between being bowled and being caught?

Sample answer

Both end the batter's turn, but they happen in different ways.

Think one step further

Which dismissal do you think is the most exciting to watch? Why?

Reading

Part 6: The Three Formats

Cricket has three main formats. A Test match is the longest.

Each team can bat in two innings, and the match can last up to five days. A One Day International, called an ODI, gives each team 50 overs to bat.

An ODI usually finishes in one day. A Twenty20, called a T20, gives each team only 20 overs.

A T20 match takes about three hours. The ICC Cricket World Cup uses ODI matches. The ICC Men’s T20 World Cup uses T20 matches.

Vocabulary

Useful Topic Vocabulary

wicket
three wooden stumps with two bails on top; the target the bowler aims atThe ball hit the wicket and the bails fell off.
over
a set of six balls bowled by one bowlerThe bowler took two wickets in one over.
runs
points scored by the batting teamThe team scored 250 runs in their innings.
innings
the period when one team bats until ten players are outEngland scored 300 runs in their first innings.
boundary
the rope around the edge of the field; hitting it scores four or six runsThe ball rolled to the boundary for four runs.
dismissal
a way a batter gets out and their innings endsBeing caught is one of the most common dismissals.
Test match
the longest format of cricket, lasting up to five daysThe Test match between Australia and England lasted four days.
T20
the shortest main format, with twenty overs per teamThe T20 match finished in less than three hours.
