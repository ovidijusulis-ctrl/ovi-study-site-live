A Day for Kids in the Netherlands

Quality checked lesson

Published 2 Nov 2026

Children in the Netherlands eat chocolate on bread, ride bikes, learn to swim and have fun special days like Sinterklaas and King's Day.

Introduction

Let's spend one day with children in the Netherlands. First, we eat breakfast.

Then we go to school by bike. We eat lunch at school.

We play after school. We eat dinner with the family. At the end, we visit two special days: Sinterklaas and King's Day.

Before you begin

Warm-up

  1. What do you eat for breakfast?

  2. How do you go to school?

  3. What is your favorite special day?

Reading

Part 1: Morning: Chocolate on Bread

It is seven o'clock. The children get up and go to the kitchen. Breakfast is bread. On the bread, there is butter. On the butter, there is hagelslag. Hagelslag is very small pieces of chocolate. The butter helps the chocolate stay on the bread. Dutch children love it!

On other days, they eat cheese on their bread. Why bread? Dutch people often eat bread in the morning and again at lunch. The hot food comes in the evening.

Reading

Part 2: Going to School by Bike

Many Dutch children go to school by bike. Why? Most of the country is flat, like a table. There are not many big hills, so riding is easy. Many streets also have a special place only for bikes. Small children ride with Mom or Dad. Big children ride alone.

At about ten years old, children take a bike test. They ride on real streets, and people watch them. Why a test? There are cars on the street, so children must ride well.

Reading

Part 3: Lunch at School

School starts at half past eight. Children bring a lunch box from home. In the box, there is bread again, maybe with cheese. Why from home? Most Dutch schools do not cook lunch. The children eat in the classroom with their teacher. Then they play outside. In Japan, the school gives children a hot lunch.

Birthdays are fun at school. The birthday child brings something small to eat. It is for all the children in the class. It is often fruit. Why does the birthday child give food? In the Netherlands, on your birthday, you give to other people. You do not only get.

Reading

Part 4: After School: Water and Ice

After school, children play outside with friends. They also go to swimming lessons. Why? The Netherlands has water everywhere: rivers, canals and the sea. A canal is a long river made by people. Near all this water, children must swim well.

For the first swimming test, children swim in their clothes and shoes. Why clothes? Sometimes a child goes into the water with clothes on. Then the child must swim in clothes!

In a very cold winter, the water turns to ice. Then families go on the ice with special shoes. This is ice skating. Children love it.

Reading

Part 6: Special Days: Sinterklaas and King's Day

In November, Sinterklaas comes to the Netherlands. He is an old man with a long white beard. He wears red clothes. In the story, he comes from Spain on a big boat. He rides a white horse.

At night, children put one shoe near the fire or the window. They put a carrot in the shoe for the horse. Why? In the story, the horse runs over the houses all night. It gets hungry! In the morning, the carrot is gone. In the shoe, there are sweets or a small present. On 5 December, children get their big presents.

On 27 April, it is King's Day. It is the king's birthday. Everyone wears orange. Why orange? The king's family has the name Orange. Children sell their old toys on the street. On other days, people must ask the town first. On King's Day, they do not need to ask!
