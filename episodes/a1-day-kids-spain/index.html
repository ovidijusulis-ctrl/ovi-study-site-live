A Day for Kids in Spain

Quality checked lesson

Published 2 Nov 2026

Kids in Spain eat late, play outside until late, and have fun special days like Three Kings Day.

Introduction

Let's spend one day with kids in Spain! In the morning, we eat breakfast and go to school.

In the afternoon, we eat a big lunch and a snack. In the evening, we play outside and see some basketball.

At night, we eat a late dinner. Then we see three special days.

One is Three Kings Day. One is your birthday. One is the night a tooth comes out.

Before you begin

Warm-up

  1. What do you eat for breakfast?

  2. What time do you eat dinner?

  3. Where do you play after school?

Reading

Part 1: Morning: Breakfast and School

The day starts with breakfast. Breakfast in Spain is small. Many children eat toast and drink juice. Some children drink Colacao. Colacao is a chocolate milk drink.

School starts at nine o'clock. In the morning, children have a break. They eat a snack, a little food. Many children bring fruit or a bocadillo. A bocadillo is a big sandwich. It often has ham or cheese inside.

Why do they eat at school? Lunch in Spain is late, so children get hungry!

Part 2: Afternoon: A Big Lunch

Many children finish school at two o'clock. They go home for lunch. Other children eat lunch at school. Then they have more classes. They go home at half past four.

Lunch is at about two o'clock. It is the biggest meal of the day. Children eat meat, fish or rice. Then they eat dessert. Lunch time is long. It can be two or three hours! Lunch is the big family meal.

At five o'clock, children are hungry again. Now it is time for merienda. Merienda is the afternoon snack. Children eat a bocadillo with meat. Or they have milk and cookies. Why another snack? Dinner in Spain is very late.

Part 3: Evening: Games and Basketball

After merienda, children go to the plaza. A plaza is a big open place in a town. Many plazas have a playground. Children run and play there. Parents sit and talk with friends. They watch all the children together.

In summer, the day in Spain is very hot. The evening is cool. So children play outside until late.

Spain loves basketball. Basketball clubs are all over Spain. Children from 8 to 14 learn with a coach. A coach is a basketball teacher. They learn to run with the ball and throw it. Small children play minibasket. In minibasket, the basket is not so high. So small children can get the ball in.

Why do so many kids play? Spain was number one in the world in 2006. It was number one again in 2019. Children watched the team win. Then many children wanted to play too. One big star is Pau Gasol from Barcelona. He is 2.13 meters tall!

Part 4: Night: A Late Dinner

Part 5: Three Kings Day

Children in Spain get big presents on January 6. Who brings them? The Three Kings! In an old story, three kings come on camels. They bring presents to baby Jesus.

On the evening of January 5, big towns have a parade. A parade is a long line of people in the street. There is music, and there are big cars. The kings are in the parade, and they throw candy. Many children bring an umbrella. Why? They open it and hold it with the top down. Now it is like a big cup. They can catch lots of candy!

At night, children put their shoes by the door. The kings leave presents there. Children leave a drink for each king. They leave food and water for the animals too. The animals walk a long way, so they are hungry!

In the morning, children find their presents. Families eat roscón. Roscón is a big cake with a hole in it. A little toy is inside. Find the toy, and you are king or queen for the day! Find a bean, and you buy the cake one year later.

Reading

Part 6: Birthdays and Teeth

On your birthday in Spain, your family pulls your ears! They do it one time for every year. Are you seven? Then you get seven! Why? People say ears grow all your life. So it means: have a long life!

When a tooth comes out, children in Spain wait for a mouse. His name is Ratoncito Pérez. The child puts the tooth under the pillow. At night, the mouse takes the tooth. He leaves a little money, candy or a small present.

Why a mouse? A long time ago, a boy king lived in Spain. His tooth came out. He was eight years old. His mother asked a writer for a story. The writer made a story about a little mouse. In the story, the mouse lives in a box of cookies. The box is in a house in Madrid.
