Introduction

What do kids in the USA eat for breakfast? How do they go to school?

Many kids ride a big yellow bus. At school, they read, write, play and eat lunch with friends.

After school, they play and do homework. You can follow one example day from morning to night. You can compare it with your day too.

  2. How do you go to school?

Reading

Part 1: Morning at Home and the Trip to School

This is an example day. Every family is different. Every school is different too.

It is seven o'clock in the morning. A clock rings. Many children wake up at home. They get out of bed and wash their face. Then they get dressed. Some schools have a uniform. Next, children brush their teeth.

Then it is time for breakfast. Many children eat cereal with milk. Cereal is small, dry pieces of grain in a bowl. Some children eat eggs and toast. Toast is hot, brown bread. Some children eat fruit, like a banana or an apple. Some children drink orange juice or milk. Some children eat breakfast at school. Some children can get school breakfast for free.

After breakfast, children pack a bag. A school bag is called a backpack. They put in books, pencils and a water bottle. Some children put a lunch box in too.

Now they go to school. Some children walk with a parent. Some children go by car. Many children ride a yellow school bus. The bus stops near their home. Children get on the bus and sit with friends. The bus arrives at school. Children get off and say hello to their teacher.

Reading

Part 2: Morning Classes and Lunch

Children go into their classroom. In about half of the states, children say the Pledge of Allegiance every day at school. This is a short promise to the country. Children stand and look at the flag.

Then classes start. First, children have reading. The teacher reads a story out loud. Children listen. Then children read books too. Some children read alone. Some children read in small groups.

Next is writing. The teacher walks around and helps them. Then they have math. Children count, add and take away. They use numbers, blocks and pictures. The teacher writes on the board. Children raise their hand with questions.

In the middle of the day, children have a play break. In the USA, this break is called recess. Children go outside. They run, jump and play games. Some children play on swings.

Then it is lunchtime. Many schools have a big eating room. It is called the cafeteria. It has long tables and many chairs. Children sit and eat with friends. Some children bring lunch from home. A lunch box can have a sandwich, an apple, carrots or crackers. Other children get school lunch. School lunch can be pizza, chicken, rice, salad or fruit. Many children drink milk. Some children get school meals for free or at a lower price.

Check: Part 2: Morning Classes and Lunch

1. Where do many children eat lunch, and what can they eat?

Sample answer

Many children eat lunch in the cafeteria with friends. They can eat a sandwich from home, or a school lunch like pizza, chicken or salad.

Useful language

Useful phrases

Can I sit here?
Use it at a table with free chairs. It is a polite way to join people.Can I sit here? This chair is free.
What do you have for lunch?
Use it to ask a friend about their food.What do you have for lunch today?
I have a sandwich and an apple.
Use it to talk about your food. Change the food words.I have rice and an orange.

Reading

Part 3: Afternoon Classes and Going Home

After lunch, classes start again. The afternoon can have different subjects. A subject is one part of learning, like math or art.

Some days, children have art. They draw pictures. They paint with many colors. They cut paper and make things.

Some days, they have music. Children sing songs together. Some children play small drums or bells.

Some days, they have PE. PE is sports class. Children go to the gym. A gym is a big room for sports. They run, throw balls and play team games.

Some days, they have science. Children look at plants, water or small animals. They ask questions. Why does ice melt? Why do plants need water?

At the end of the day, children tidy up. They put their books away. They clean their desks. Some children put their chairs on the desks. Then they get their backpacks and coats.

The school day ends in the afternoon. Many children go home on the yellow bus. Some parents come by car. Some children walk home with an adult.

Reading

Part 4: After School

Many children are hungry after school. They eat a snack at home. A snack is a small food between meals. It can be an apple, cheese, crackers or a cookie. They drink water or milk.

Then many children do homework. Homework is school work at home. It can be a short book or a few math questions. A parent can help.

After that, children can play. Some children play outside with friends. They ride bikes. They play ball in the yard or at the park. A yard is the grass next to a house.

Many children do activities after school. Some children play sports, like soccer, basketball or baseball. Some children go to music or art class. Some children go to a club. A club is a group with the same hobby. Some children go to an after-school program at their school. There, they get help with reading and math. They play games there too.

Some children visit their grandparents. Some children play with brothers, sisters or friends.

Check: Part 4: After School

1. What can children do after school?

Sample answer

They can eat a snack, do homework and play outside. They can also play sports, go to a club or visit family.

Useful language

Useful phrases

Do you ride the bus?
Use it to ask how a friend goes to school.Do you ride the bus or walk?
What do you do after school?
Use it to ask about a friend's afternoon.What do you do after school on Mondays?
See you in class!
Use it when you leave a friend. You meet them again soon.Bye, Tom! See you in class!

Reading

Part 5: Dinner, Bedtime and the Weekend

In the evening, the family eats dinner. Dinner can be pasta, chicken, rice, tacos or soup with vegetables. Some families eat together at the table. Children talk about their day.

Children help at home too. They put plates and forks on the table. After dinner, they take their plates to the sink. Some children feed the dog or the cat.

Then children have free time. They play with toys, draw or watch TV. Some children play video games.

Before bed, children wash. They take a bath or a shower. They put on pajamas. Pajamas are soft clothes for sleeping. Then they brush their teeth again.

Many parents read a story at bedtime. Children listen in bed. Then the lights go off. Most children have a regular bedtime. This means the same bedtime on most nights. Young children often have a regular bedtime. Older children have one less often.

On the weekend, there is no school. Children can sleep a little longer. They play, see family and go to sports games.

Conversation: Lunch with a New Friend in the Cafeteria

You

Hi! Can I sit here?

Mia

Sure! Sit next to me. I'm Mia.

You

Thank you. I'm from Japan. I'm here for one day.

Mia

Wow, Japan! Welcome to our school. Do you like it?

You

Yes! I like recess. We ran and played tag.

Mia

Me too. Recess is my favorite. What do you have for lunch?

You

I have rice balls and some fruit. What about you?

Mia

I have a sandwich and an apple. My mom makes it. Sometimes I get school lunch. Today it is pizza.

You

Pizza at school? That's cool. At my school in Japan, we eat in our classroom.

Mia

Really? Here we eat in the cafeteria. It is big and loud!

You

Yes, it is very loud! Do you ride the bus?

Mia

Yes, I ride the yellow bus. My stop is near my house. How do you go to school?

You

I walk to school with my friends. What do you do after school?

Mia

First, I eat a snack. Then I do my homework. On Tuesdays, I play soccer.

You

I play soccer too!

Mia

Great! Maybe we can play soccer in PE this afternoon.

You

I hope so!

Mia

Oh, that's the bell. Lunch is over. Put your trash here.

You

Okay. Thank you, Mia. See you in class!

Mia

See you in class! Let's go.

Speak

Speaking

Tell a partner about your day. Use short sentences, from morning to night.

1. Say what time you wake up and what you eat for breakfast.

2. Say how you go to school.

3. Name your classes in the morning and the afternoon.

4. Say what you eat for lunch and where you eat it.

5. Say what you do after school and before bed.

Then ask your partner the same questions. Find two things that are the same in your days. Find one thing that is different from a day in the USA.

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Kids in the USA have a busy day, from cereal in the morning to a story at night. Many details change from family to family and from school to school. Some parts may look like your day, and some parts may look new.

  1. Which American breakfast do you want to try?

  2. Do you want to ride a yellow school bus? Why?

  3. Which part of the American school day do you like best?

  4. What do you do after school that American kids do too?
