Reading

Part 1: Morning at Home and the Trip to School

It is seven o'clock in the morning. A clock rings. Children wake up and get out of bed. They wash their face and put on their clothes. Then they brush their teeth.

Now it is time for breakfast. Children eat cereal with milk, or eggs and toast. They put the cereal in a bowl and add milk. Toast is hot, brown bread. Fruit is good for breakfast too. Children eat a banana or an apple. They drink orange juice or milk. Breakfast gives them food before a busy day at school.

After breakfast, children get their school bag. It is called a backpack. They put in books, pencils and a water bottle. They can put a lunch box in too. Now they are ready to go.

Children go to school by bus or by car. They can walk too. The yellow school bus stops near their home. Children get on the bus and sit with friends. They look out of the window. The bus arrives at school. Children get off and say hello to their teacher.

Reading

Part 2: Morning Classes and Lunch

Children go into their classroom. They say hello to their friends. They put their bags away and sit at their desks.

First, they have reading. The teacher reads a story to the class. Children listen and look at the book. Then they read too. They read alone or in small groups. They talk about the story with their teacher.

Next is writing. The teacher walks around and helps the children. Then they have math. They work with numbers. They add and take away. They use blocks and pictures too. The teacher writes on the board. Children put a hand up to ask a question.

After class, it is time to play. This time is called recess. Children go outside. They run, jump and play games with friends.

Then it is time for lunch. The school has a big eating room called the cafeteria. It has long tables and many chairs. Children sit and eat with friends. They bring lunch from home or get school lunch. A lunch box can have a sandwich, an apple or cheese. School lunch can be pizza, chicken, rice, salad or fruit. Many children drink milk. School meals can be free.

Check: Part 2: Morning Classes and Lunch

1. Where do many children eat lunch, and what can they eat?

Sample answer

Many children eat lunch in the cafeteria with friends. They can eat a sandwich from home, or a school lunch like pizza, chicken or salad.

Useful language

Useful phrases

Can I sit here?
Use it at a table with free chairs. It is a polite way to join people.Can I sit here? This chair is free.
What do you have for lunch?
Use it to ask a friend about their food.What do you have for lunch today?
I have a sandwich and an apple.
Use it to talk about your food. Change the food words.I have rice and an orange.

Reading

Part 3: Afternoon Classes and Going Home

After lunch, classes start again. Children have more to learn and do. There is time for art, music, games and science.

In art class, children draw pictures. They paint with many colors. They cut paper and make things. They show their pictures to the teacher.

In music class, children sing songs together. They listen to music and play small drums or bells.

PE is sports class. Children go to a big room for games. They run, throw balls and play in teams. They can play outside too.

In science class, children learn about water and small animals. They look at pictures and ask questions. The teacher helps them find answers.

At the end of the day, children clean the classroom. They put their books away and clean their desks. They get their bags and coats. Now it is time to go home.

The school day ends in the afternoon. Children go home on the yellow bus, walk with a parent or go by car. They say bye to their teacher and friends.

Reading

Part 4: After School

Children are hungry after school. At home, they eat a snack. A snack is a little food between meals. It can be an apple, cheese or a cookie. They drink water or milk.

Then it is time for homework. Homework is school work at home. Children read a little book or answer math questions. A parent can help. Children put their books away when they finish.

After that, there is time to play. Children go outside with friends. They ride bikes and play ball. They play in the yard or at the park. A yard is the grass next to a house.

Children can do activities after school too. They play soccer, basketball or baseball. They go to music or art class. They can join a club with other children who like the same things.

An after-school program is a place to go after class. Children get help with reading and math there. They play games too. After school is also a time to see grandparents, brothers, sisters and friends.

Reading

Part 5: Dinner, Bed and the Weekend

In the evening, the family eats dinner. Dinner can be chicken, rice or soup with vegetables. Children sit at the table and talk about their day. They talk about school, friends and games.

Children help at home too. They put things on the table for dinner. After dinner, they help clean the table. They give food to the dog or the cat.

Then there is free time. Children play with toys, draw or watch TV. They can play video games too.

Before bed, children wash. They take a bath or a shower. They put on clean clothes for bed. Then they brush their teeth again.

A parent reads a story. Children listen in bed and look at the pictures in the book. Then they say good night. The lights go off. It is time to sleep.

On the weekend, there is no school. Children can sleep a little longer. They play, see family and go to sports games.

Roleplay

Conversation: Lunch with a New Friend in the Cafeteria

You

Hi! Can I sit here?

Mia

Sure! Sit next to me. I'm Mia.

You

Thank you. I'm from Japan. I'm here for one day.

Mia

Wow, Japan! Welcome to our school. Do you like it?

You

Yes! I like recess. We ran and played tag.

Mia

Me too. Recess is my favorite. What do you have for lunch?

You

I have rice balls and some fruit. What about you?

Mia

I have a sandwich and an apple. My mom makes it. Sometimes I get school lunch. Today it is pizza.

You

Pizza at school? That's cool. At my school in Japan, we eat in our classroom.

Mia

Really? Here we eat in the cafeteria. It is big and loud!

You

Yes, it is very loud! Do you ride the bus?

Mia

Yes, I ride the yellow bus. My stop is near my house. How do you go to school?

You

I walk to school with my friends. What do you do after school?

Mia

First, I eat a snack. Then I do my homework. On Tuesdays, I play soccer.

You

I play soccer too!

Mia

Great! Maybe we can play soccer in PE this afternoon.

You

I hope so!

Mia

Oh, that's the bell. Lunch is over. Put your trash here.

You

Okay. Thank you, Mia. See you in class!

Mia

See you in class! Let's go.

Speak

Speaking

Tell a partner about your day. Use short sentences, from morning to night.

1. Say what time you wake up and what you eat for breakfast.

2. Say how you go to school.

3. Name your classes in the morning and the afternoon.

4. Say what you eat for lunch and where you eat it.

5. Say what you do after school and before bed.

Then ask your partner the same questions. Find two things that are the same in your days. Find one thing that is different from a day in the USA.

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Kids in the USA eat, learn and play. They have breakfast, go to school and eat lunch with friends. After school, there is time for homework and games. The day ends with dinner and a story.

  1. Which American breakfast do you want to try?

  2. Do you want to ride a yellow school bus? Why?

  3. Which part of the American school day do you like best?

  4. What do you do after school that American kids do too?
