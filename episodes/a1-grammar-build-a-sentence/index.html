Reading

Part 1: Am, Is and Are

Emma and Ben are in class. Be means am, is or are. It tells who, how old, where or how someone is.

PersonBeShort form
IamI'm
he, she, itishe's, she's, it's
you, we, theyareyou're, we're, they're

Rule: Match am, is or are to the person.

  • I am Ben. I'm at school.
  • Is Emma ready? Yes, she is. / No, she isn't. (Emma = she)
  • Are you happy? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
  • Ben is not tired. He isn't tired. (Ben = he)
  • We are at school. They are at home.

Watch out:

  • Wrong: She are ready. Right: She is ready. Use is with he, she and it.
  • Are you happy? Wrong: Yes, you are. Right: Yes, I am. Answer about yourself with I. Answer about your group with we: Yes, we are.

    Part 2: English Sentence Order

    Emma and Ben talk in class. English words go in order. The verb is be or a doing word, like read.

    PersonVerbMore words
    Benishappy.
    Wereadbooks.
    BePersonMore words

    |---|---|---|

    | Is | Ben | happy? |

    | Are | they | at school? |

    Rule: The person comes first, then the verb. In a question, be comes before the person. Where comes first, then is or are, then the person.

    • Ben is happy.
    • We read. This short sentence is OK.
    • They play games after school.
    • Is Ben happy? Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
    • They are not at school.
    • Where is Emma? She is in the park.

    Watch out:

    • Wrong: Happy is Ben. Right: Ben is happy. The person comes first.
    • Wrong: Do you are ready? Right: Are you ready? Never use do with am, is or are.

    Study: Look at these sentences. What moves?

    • Emma is at school.
    • Is Emma at school?

      Part 3: Be or an Action Verb

      Ben is at the door. Be tells who, how old, where or how someone is. An action verb is a doing word, like walk, eat or play.

      BeDoing word
      I am tired.I eat lunch.
      We are at school.We walk to school.
      They are happy.They play games.

      Rule: Use be or a doing word, not both.

      • I am tired. I go to bed.
      • Is Ben at the door? Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
      • Emma and Ben aren't tired. They play games.
      • They read books after school.

      Watch out:

      • Wrong: I am go to school. Right: I go to school. Go is the doing word. Am is extra.
      • Wrong: She happy. Right: She is happy. Happy is not a doing word.

        Part 4: Capital Letters and Sentence Endings

        Our teacher writes on the board. A capital letter is a big letter, like A or B.

        SentenceEnds withExample
        tellingfull stop .Emma is here.
        askingquestion mark ?Is Emma here?

        Rule: Start with a capital letter. End with a full stop or a question mark.

        • I am here.
        • Emma and I are friends.
        • Is Ben ready? Yes, he is.
        • They are not late.

        Watch out:

        • Wrong: are you ready. Right: Are you ready? A question ends with a question mark.
        • Wrong: ben and i play. Right: Ben and I play. Names and I need a capital letter.
        • Wrong: Weareready. Right: We are ready. Put one space between words.

        Spelling:

        • Always write I as a capital letter.
        • Names start with a capital letter: Emma, Ben.

          Speaking

          Work with a partner. Ask a question with be, like Are you tired? Your partner says Yes, I am or No, I'm not. Say a sentence with not, like I'm not tired. Then tell your partner about a classmate: She is happy. He isn't here. Change roles.

          What you can do now

          Final Reflection

          Now you can make and write short sentences and ask questions.

          1. Which sentence can you write now?

          2. Can you make it a question?

          3. Can you say it with not?
