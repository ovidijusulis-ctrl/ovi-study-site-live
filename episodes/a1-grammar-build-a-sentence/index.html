Reading

Part 1: Am, Is and Are

You meet someone at school.

Be tells us who or where someone is.

It also tells us how someone feels.

Use am with I.

Use is with he, she and it.

Use are with you, we and they.

I am a student.

Emma is a teacher. She is a teacher.

Ben is here. He is here.

We are at school.

It is sunny.

Use it for the weather.

For questions, put am, is or are first.

You are ready.

Are you ready?

Yes, I am.

No, I am not.

Answer with I when someone asks about you.

To say no, add not after be.

I am not ready.

Where is Emma?

She is at school.

Short forms:

I'm = I am.

Isn't = is not.

Aren't = are not.

Keep am in Yes, I am.

Wrong: Do you are ready?

Write Are you ready? Put be first.

Study: Read these two sentences.

You are ready.

Are you ready?

Which words change places?

      Reading

      Part 3: Be or an Action Verb

      Be tells who, where or how.

      An action verb tells what someone does.

      I am ready.

      I walk to school.

      We are happy.

      We play football.

      I read books at home.

      Walk, play and read show actions.

      Use be or an action verb in these sentences.

      Wrong: I am go to school.

      Write I go to school.

      Go is the verb here.

      Do not add am before go.

      Study: Read I am ready and I walk.

      Which sentence has an action?
