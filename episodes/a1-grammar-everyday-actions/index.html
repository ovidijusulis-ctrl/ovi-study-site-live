Reading

Part 1: Everyday Actions

Use the present simple for things you do often.

Put I, you, we, or they before read or play.

Read stays the same with these four words.

I read every day.

You eat breakfast every day.

We walk to school on Mondays.

They play at home after school.

Read is an ordinary verb: it tells us an action.

Ready tells us how someone is.

I read. I am ready.

Use am with ready.

Say I read, not I am read.

Say one true sentence about your day.

    For questions, put do before I, you, we, or they.

    Then add read, eat, walk, or play.

    For no, put do not before read or play.

    You changes to I when you give your answer.

    Do they walk to school?

    Yes, they do. / No, they do not.

    Short answers use do.

    Do not add read or walk after do.

    Put what, where, or when before do.

    What do you read? Books.

    Where do you read? At home.

    What asks about things; where asks about places.

    When asks about time.

    Are you ready? uses be, so leave out do.

      Reading

      Part 3: Don't and Do Not

      Don't and do not mean the same thing.

      Put either before read, eat, walk, or play.

      I do not play after school.

      I don't play after school.

      You don't read at home.

      We don't walk to school.

      They don't eat breakfast at home.

      In don't, the letter o from not is not there.

      An apostrophe shows this.

      Put the apostrophe before t.

      Write don't as one word.

      Dont is wrong: there is no apostrophe.
