Reading

Part 1: Everyday Actions

You talk about your day with a friend. For things that happen often, use the present simple.

PersonVerb
Iread every day
youeat breakfast
wewalk to school on Mondays
theyplay after school

Rule: After I, you, we and they, the verb stays the same.

  • I do my homework after school.
  • I like cats. We need water.
  • I read books. I am ready.

Like and need are verbs too. Ordinary verbs use do to ask and say no, not be.

Watch out: Not I am read. Say I read.

    Part 2: Questions and Negatives with Do

    Two friends talk about home.

    PersonDo questionShort answers
    you (only you)Do you read at home?Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
    you (a group)Do you read at home?Yes, we do. / No, we don't.
    theyDo they walk to school?Yes, they do. / No, they don't.

    Rule: To ask, put do first. To say no, put do not before the verb.

    • You read at home.
    • Do you read at home?
    • I do not read at home.
    • They don't walk to school.

    You means only you? Answer with I. A group? Answer with we.

    A question word comes first, then do.

    Question wordAsks aboutExample
    Whata thingWhat do you read?
    Wherea placeWhere do you eat lunch?
    Whena timeWhen do you read?

    Be is different: Are you ready?

    Watch out: Not Are you read? Say Do you read?

      Part 3: Don't and Do Not

      Your answer is no. Write do not or don't.

      Full formShort form
      I do not play.I don't play.
      They do not eat.They don't eat.

      Rule: Don't and do not mean the same thing.

      • We do not walk to school.
      • We don't walk to school.
      • I don't like cats.
      • Do you play? No, I don't.

      In don't, the o from not is gone. The apostrophe is in its place.

      Watch out: Not dont. Say don't.
