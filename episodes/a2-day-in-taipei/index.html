Reading

Part 1: From the Airport to the City

Your plane lands at Taoyuan International Airport, near Taipei. The easiest way into the city is the Airport MRT. MRT is the city's train system, like a subway. The express train takes about 35 minutes to Taipei Main Station. One ride costs NT$160 (New Taiwan dollars).

Before you get on the train, buy an EasyCard. An EasyCard is a travel card. You put money on it. Then you touch it on the ticket gate. It works on the MRT, on the bus and in many shops. In Japan, Suica works the same way.

The card costs NT$100, but this money is only for the card. A new card has no money for rides. So you add money at the same time. This is called topping up. You can buy a card and top it up in these places.

  • 7-Eleven and FamilyMart shops in the airport
  • the information desk at the Airport MRT station
  • the information desk in any MRT station

In Taipei, one MRT ride costs NT$20 to NT$65. The price depends on how far you go.

One MRT rule surprises many visitors. Past the yellow line in front of the ticket gates, you cannot eat or drink. No food, no drinks, not even water. The rule is also for the trains and the waiting areas.

Why is the rule so strict? With no food, trains and stations stay clean. If you break the rule, the fine is NT$1,500 to NT$7,500. So finish your coffee before you cross the yellow line.

Part 2: Breakfast and Bubble Tea

Now you are in the city, and you are hungry. Many people in Taiwan do not make breakfast at home. They buy it at a small breakfast shop. These shops open very early. The food is fast and cheap. So people eat it on the way to work or school.

Try these two favourites.

  • dan bing: a thin, flat bread cooked with an egg. It is rolled up and cut into pieces. It often has cheese, ham or corn in it.
  • soy milk: a drink made from beans. You can have it hot or cold, sweet or not sweet.

A simple breakfast like this costs about NT$60 to NT$120.

After breakfast, it is time for a drink. Bubble tea comes from Taiwan, and there is a tea shop on almost every street.

The most famous drink is pearl milk tea. It is milk tea with soft balls at the bottom. People call them pearls. They are made from tapioca. Tapioca is made from the root of a plant.

Here is the surprising part. In Taiwan, you choose how sweet your drink is. You also choose how much ice is in it. The staff will ask you, so have your answer ready.

Sugar levelHow sweet
regular sugar100%
less sugar70%
half sugar50%
light sugar30%
no sugar0%

The ice levels are regular ice, less ice, no ice and hot.

Why so many choices? Regular sugar is very sweet, so many local people choose half sugar or light sugar. Many also ask for less ice. Ice turns into water and makes the tea weak. With less ice, the tea is stronger.

A good first order is pearl milk tea, half sugar, less ice. Most cups cost NT$45 to NT$75.

Part 3: Taipei 101

In the afternoon, take the Red Line to Taipei 101/World Trade Center Station. Taipei 101 is the tallest building in Taiwan: 508 metres high, with 101 floors. When it opened about twenty years ago, it was the tallest building in the world.

The observatory is on the 89th floor, about 380 metres above the street. An observatory is a high place for looking at the view. From the windows, you see the city and the green mountains around it.

  • Open: every day, 10:00 am to 9:00 pm
  • Last entry: 45 minutes before closing, at 8:15 pm
  • Adult ticket: NT$600

Many visitors go up just before the sun goes down. This way, they see the city in daylight and then with all its night lights.

Near the top of the tower, there is a very big gold ball. It is called a damper. It weighs 660 tonnes and is between the 87th and 92nd floors. Visitors can see it from the observatory.

Why does a building need a very big ball? Taiwan has very strong storms and earthquakes. When strong wind pushes the tower one way, the ball moves the other way. This makes the tower move less, so people at the top feel safe and comfortable.

Part 4: Raohe Night Market

In the evening, take the Green Line to Songshan Station and leave by Exit 5. Raohe Night Market is a short walk from there. It is one long street, about 600 metres, full of food stalls. It opens at about 5 p.m. and closes at midnight, every day.

A night market is a street market that opens in the evening. People go there for dinner: they walk, talk with friends and try many small dishes. Each dish is small and cheap. So you can try five or six things in one night.

The most famous food here is the black pepper bun. It is a small round bun. It has pork, green onion and a lot of black pepper in it. The cook puts it on the wall of a very hot clay oven. It gets brown and crispy.

Each one costs about NT$65. The most famous stall is Fuzhou Shizu Black Pepper Bun. It is at the start of the market. It is in the Michelin Guide, a famous book about good food. So people often wait in line for 20 to 30 minutes.

Next, try herbal pork rib soup. The meat cooks slowly in a dark soup. The soup has herbs: plants that people also use as medicine. Many people believe this soup makes you warm. So it is popular on cool nights. A bowl costs about NT$100.

Ordering food here is easy:

  • Point at the food and say how many.
  • Say if you want to eat here or take it away.
  • Pay the person at the front.
  • Step to the side to eat, so other people can order.

Most food places at the night market take only cash. Bring small notes, like NT$100 notes. A busy stall often has little change, so a NT$1,000 note is a problem.

Conversation: Buying an EasyCard

Staff

Good morning. How can I help you?

You

Hi. I'd like to buy an EasyCard, please.

Staff

Sure. The card is NT$100. That money is only for the card. There is nothing on it for rides.

You

OK. I'd like to top up my card with NT$500.

Staff

No problem. That's NT$600, please.

You

Can I pay by credit card?

Staff

Sorry, you can only pay cash here.

You

OK. Here's NT$1,000.

Staff

Thank you. Here's your card and NT$400 change. Is this your first time in Taiwan?

You

Yes, it is. I'm from Japan, and I'm here on holiday.

Staff

Welcome! Where are you going first?

You

Taipei Main Station. How do I get there from here?

Staff

Take the express train. It takes about 35 minutes, and it's NT$160. Just touch your card at the gate.

You

Great. This afternoon I want to see Taipei 101. How do I get to Taipei 101 from Taipei Main Station?

Staff

Take the Red Line toward Guangci/Fengtian Temple. Get off at Taipei 101/World Trade Center. It's seven stops.

You

And tonight I want to go to Raohe Night Market. Where do I change trains for Songshan Station?

Staff

From Taipei 101, take the Red Line back to Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall. Change there to the Green Line. Songshan Station is the last stop.

You

Which exit is closest to the night market?

Staff

Exit 5. The market is just a short walk from there.

You

Thank you. One more question. Can I drink my coffee on the train?

Staff

Sorry, no. You can't eat or drink past the yellow line in front of the gates. The fine is NT$1,500 to NT$7,500.

You

OK. Then I'll finish it here.

Staff

Good idea. Enjoy your day in Taipei!

You

Thanks very much!
