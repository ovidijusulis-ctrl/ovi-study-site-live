Part 1: A Lake in the Middle of Canberra

Canberra is the capital of Australia, and it is a planned city. People drew the city on paper first, and then they built it.

Why? When Australia became one country, Sydney and Melbourne both wanted to be the capital. They could not agree. So the government built a new city between them.

Two Americans, a husband and wife, won a competition to draw the new city. They were Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony Griffin. For the Griffins, a lake was the heart of the city.

Why a lake? The river was in low land between hills. Water could fill this low land easily and cheaply, and a lake would be beautiful. So the Griffins drew the city around it.

But Canberra had only a small river. So workers built a dam, a big wall that stops a river. In 1963, they closed its gates. The river water stopped at the wall, and slowly it made a lake.

Its name is Lake Burley Griffin. Today the lake is the weekend park for the city.

  • People walk or ride bikes on the bridge-to-bridge loop. It is a 5-kilometre circle. It goes over two bridges.
  • Rowing clubs train in long, thin boats at the west end.
  • Families cook lunch on the free electric barbecues by the water.

Part 2: The Sausage Sizzle and Election Day

A sausage sizzle is a barbecue that sells food to raise money for a group. Sizzle is the sound of food cooking on a hot plate. You get one sausage in a slice of white bread. Many people add fried onions and tomato sauce.

The most famous ones are outside Bunnings, a big hardware shop. A hardware shop sells tools and things for your house and garden. On weekends, a local group, like a school or a sports club, cooks there.

Bunnings takes no money from the group, so the group keeps it all. A sausage costs $3.50.

Sausage sizzles also appear on election day, the day people vote. They are at polling places, the places where people vote. Why there?

In Australia, voting is compulsory. Every Australian citizen aged 18 or older must vote. People who do not vote can get a fine. A fine is money you pay when you break a rule.

Because of this law, almost everyone votes. In 2025, 90.7% of enrolled voters voted. Enrolled voters are the people whose names are on the list of voters.

Elections are on Saturdays, and people often vote in schools. Millions of people come on the day, so school parents cook for the long lines. The money helps the school. Australians call this a democracy sausage. In a democracy, people choose their leaders by voting.

Part 3: The Melbourne Cup

The Melbourne Cup is the most famous horse race in Australia. In a race, horses run to see who is fastest. People call this one the race that stops a nation. For a few minutes, many Australians stop to watch.

  • When: the first Tuesday in November, at 3 pm
  • Where: Flemington Racecourse in Melbourne
  • How far: 3,200 metres

Why do so many people stop for one race? Cup Day is a public holiday in Victoria, the state where Melbourne is. People there do not go to work. They go to the race, or they watch it on TV with friends.

In other states, Cup Day is a normal work day. But many offices stop at 3 pm. People watch the race together on TV.

Many offices also have a sweep. It is a small game for the race.

  • Everyone pays the same, often a few dollars.
  • Each person takes a horse's name from a hat.
  • The person with the winning horse gets about half of the money.

You do not need to know about horses. It is all luck, so everyone in the sweep has a horse to cheer for.

In 2026, a horse called Point Of Law won. Its trainer was Ciaron Maher. A trainer gets a horse ready for races. Ciaron Maher also trained the horses in second and third place.

This was the first time in 166 races. One trainer had the first three horses.

Part 4: Work Rules That Surprise Visitors

No tips. In cafés and restaurants, people do not usually leave a tip. A tip is a little money for the person who serves you.

Why? Australian workers already get high pay. So they do not need tips to make enough money.

The lowest pay the law allows is called the minimum wage. From 1 July 2026, it is $26.44 an hour. It is for most workers aged 21 or older.

Super. From 1 July 2025, bosses must pay extra money for almost every worker. They must pay 12% of a worker's pay into a super fund. Super is short for superannuation. The fund keeps this money until you are old.

Why? So people have their own money when they stop work.

This year there is a new rule. Now the boss pays super with every payday, the day you get your pay.

Long service leave. After 10 years with the same company, workers get long service leave. It is a long holiday, and you still get your pay.

In New South Wales, the state where Sydney is, it is two months. Other states have different rules.

Why? It is a thank-you for staying with one company for a long time.

Roleplay

Conversation: Saturday at the Lake

Jack

Hey, you made it! How's the new job in Sydney?

You

Good, but busy. On Tuesday my office stops at 3 pm for a horse race.

Jack

The Melbourne Cup! Many people watch it. Are you hungry?

You

Very. Something smells good.

Jack

That's the rowing club. They're having a sausage sizzle.

You

What's it for?

Club member

We're raising money for a new boat.

You

Great. Can I get one with onions, please? And a bottle of water.

Club member

Sure. Our prices are the same as at Bunnings. The sausage is three fifty. The water is two. That's five fifty.

You

Here's six dollars. Keep the change.

Club member

Thanks, mate! The sauce is on the table.

You

Jack, was this lake always here?

Jack

No, it's not natural. Workers built a dam in 1963, and the river filled the lake.

You

Why did the people who planned the city want a lake?

Jack

The river was in low land between hills, so water could fill it cheaply. And a lake makes the city beautiful. Do you feel like a walk after lunch?

You

Sure. How long does it take to walk around it?

Jack

All of it is too far. The bridge-to-bridge loop is 5 kilometres, over two bridges. That takes about an hour.

You

Perfect. Do you feel like a barbecue tomorrow too?

Jack

Good idea. The barbecues by the water are free. I'll bring the sausages.

You

Then I'll bring the bread. Why don't we meet here at twelve?

Jack

Twelve is good. See you then.

Roleplay

Conversation: Cup Day at the Office

Chloe

There you are! We're doing a sweep. It's five dollars, and you take a horse from the hat.

You

Count me in. Here's five dollars.

Chloe

Thanks. Close your eyes and take one name.

You

I got Juja Kibo. Who did you get, Liam?

Liam

Point Of Law. It's the favourite! That means it has the best chance to win.

Chloe

Lucky you. We have twenty people, so there's a hundred dollars. First place gets fifty, second gets thirty and third gets twenty.

You

Does all of Australia really stop for this?

Liam

Not everyone, but many people do. In Melbourne it's a public holiday. Here we just stop work for about ten minutes.

Chloe

It's three o'clock. They're off!

You

Come on, Juja Kibo!

Liam

Point Of Law is in front... Yes! Point Of Law wins!

Chloe

And Juja Kibo is third! You win twenty dollars.

Liam

Look at this. The first three horses all have the same trainer, Ciaron Maher.

Chloe

That has never happened before. It's the first time in 166 races. And the rider, Ethan Brown, won the Cup for the first time.

You

Liam, you won fifty dollars. Are you buying lunch?

Liam

Maybe! I want a big holiday next year. I'm taking time off for two months.

You

Two months? How long have you been at this company?

Liam

Ten years in March. So I get long service leave: two months, with full pay.

You

Is it true that the company also pays money for when we are old?

Chloe

Yes, that's super. The company pays 12% of your pay into your super fund.

Liam

And since July, it comes with every payday, not every three months.

You

Australian work rules are pretty good!

Chloe

Not bad, right? Okay, back to work.
