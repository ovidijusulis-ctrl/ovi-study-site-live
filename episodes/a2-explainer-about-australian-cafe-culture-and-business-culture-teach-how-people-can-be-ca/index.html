Before you begin

Warm-up

  1. How often do you visit a cafe?

  2. What makes you return to the same business?

  3. Do you prefer friendly talk or very formal service?

Reading

Part 1: Cafes and Regular Customers

An Australian cafe is not only a place to buy coffee. People may meet, talk, read, or take a short break there. A regular is a customer who comes back often. Staff may remember the person’s order and ask, “The usual?” This sounds casual, but it also shows careful service.

A customer may say, “Can I get a flat white?” A flat white has espresso, steamed milk, and a thin layer of soft foam. In Australia, it often comes in a ceramic cup.

Check your understanding

Check: Part 1: Cafes and Regular Customers

1. What does “The usual?” mean in a cafe?

Sample answer

It means the staff know the regular customer’s normal order and are asking if they want it again.

Think one step further

Do you have a cafe you visit often?

Do you like it when staff remember your usual order?

What do you usually say when you walk into a cafe?

Reading

Part 2: Real Coffee Habits in 2026

Coffee remained part of daily life in Australia in 2026. The cafe and coffee shop industry had 27,821 businesses and a market size of $15.9 billion.

However, people watched their spending more carefully. In June 2026, the average cup cost $4.69. Cafe spending fell 1.4% from the year before, and 83% of surveyed cafe operators noticed fewer visits. Still, 93% of cafe spending happened in person. This gives a real business picture: many people still liked visiting their local cafe, but some went less often.

Check: Part 2: Real Coffee Habits in 2026

1. Did Australians stop caring about cafes in 2026?

Sample answer

No. Many people still valued cafe visits, but they became more careful about how often they went and what they bought.

Think one step further

Do you have a usual drink at a cafe?

Do you buy coffee often?

Do you drink coffee more at home or at a cafe?

Reading

Part 3: Casual Style, Serious Standards

Casual language does not mean careless work. Think about two things: style and standard. The style can be friendly and relaxed, while the standard for coffee, time, and service can stay high.

A customer may ask directly, “How long will it take?” A plain complaint may be, “This coffee is cold.” These words can sound strong, but they clearly show the problem. Good business service focuses on the problem and fixes it. Australian cafe culture often values drink quality more than loud marketing, so exaggerated claims may create less trust.

Check: Part 3: Casual Style, Serious Standards

1. How can a cafe be casual and serious at the same time?

Sample answer

The staff can speak in a relaxed way while still working carefully, serving good coffee, and fixing problems.

Think one step further

Do you like friendly talk from cafe staff?

Do you care more about careful service than friendly talk?

What do you do if a cafe makes a mistake with your order?

Reading

Part 4: Australia and Japan Are Closely Connected

Cafe business also sits inside a larger relationship between Australia and Japan. In 2025, Japan was Australia’s second-largest partner for merchandise and services exports, worth $65.1 billion. It was the third-largest partner for imports, worth $32.4 billion.

Travel was also strong. During 2025, 1,058,300 Australian travellers visited Japan. This was the first year the number passed one million, and it was 15% higher than in 2024. These trade and travel links help people meet new products, food ideas, and ways of doing business.

Part 5: An Honest Charcoal Latte Example

Imagine an unnamed cafe in Japan offering a black latte made with bamboo charcoal powder. Bamboo charcoal can give a drink a surprising black colour. The cafe may also explain Binchotan, a high-quality white charcoal made for a long time in Wakayama from ubame oak.

The business should be clear about the product. It can describe the colour, taste, and ingredients without calling the drink a miracle health product. Some charcoal health claims have weak evidence, and activated charcoal can bind to medicines. Customers may like the creative look, but honest information builds trust.

Check: Part 5: An Honest Charcoal Latte Example

1. Why should the cafe avoid large health claims about its charcoal latte?

Sample answer

It should avoid them because some claims have weak evidence, and charcoal can also bind to medicines.

Think one step further

Do you read drink names carefully before you order?

Do you ever try drinks with a strange color?

What do you do when a menu makes a big health claim?

Reading

Part 6: Family Craftsmanship and Quiet Quality

Picture an unnamed family workshop making Japanese roof tiles. These fired-clay tiles are called kawara. They came to Japan from Baekche during the sixth century and were first used on temples and government buildings. The Kawara Museum in Omihachiman now shows local tile history and production.

The family may make an onigawara, a roof ornament with a fierce face or beast. Instead of saying, “Our work is the best in the world,” they can show the clay, firing process, strong finished tile, and family skill. Like a good cafe, the workshop can feel friendly while taking quality seriously.
