Reading

Part 1: Dragon Ball Begins

Akira Toriyama was a Japanese manga artist. He wrote and drew the Dragon Ball manga from 1984 to 1995. Manga means a Japanese comic, and it came before the anime.

The early story follows Goku as a boy. He lives far from towns until he meets Bulma. They look for seven magical Dragon Balls. Together, the balls can call a dragon that gives one wish. Goku also trains in martial arts with Master Roshi, makes friends, and enters fighting tournaments. The first Dragon Ball anime ran from 1986 to 1989 and showed this early story.

Reading

Part 2: Goku Grows Up in Dragon Ball Z

Dragon Ball Z continued the later part of the manga. The anime ran from 1989 to 1996. It began about five years after the first anime, so Goku was now a young adult and a father. His son was named Gohan.

An alien called Raditz told Goku that he was a Saiyan, a warrior from another planet. Goku then faced much stronger enemies, including the space tyrant Frieza and the magical being Majin Buu. The story became larger: Goku and his friends often fought to defend Earth. Gohan also trained and became stronger.

Reading

Part 3: GT Takes a Separate Road

Dragon Ball GT was a Toei anime with 64 episodes. It ran from 1996 to 1997, after Z, but it was not based on Toriyama’s manga. Toriyama described it as a grand side story.

At the beginning, a wish changed adult Goku back into a child. He travelled through space with his granddaughter Pan and Trunks. They searched for the Black Star Dragon Balls because Earth would explode in one year if they failed. Later, Goku fought enemies such as Baby and the Shadow Dragons. GT was one possible continuation after Z, but it was separate from Super.

Check: Part 3: GT Takes a Separate Road

1. Why do Goku, Pan, and Trunks search for the Black Star Dragon Balls?

Sample answer

Reading

Part 4: Super Opens a Bigger Universe

Dragon Ball Super was another continuation after Z. Its anime ran from 2015 to 2018, and its story began after Majin Buu’s defeat, during the ten-year gap at the end of Z.

Super is part of the main line, meaning it is treated as the main continuation of the story. Goku met Beerus, a God of Destruction. He also learned that other universes existed. In the Tournament of Power, fighters from different universes competed against one another. Z often focused on protecting Earth, but Super made the setting much bigger by adding gods and many universes.

Reading

Part 5: How the Four Versions Differ

A simple comparison uses three ideas: starting point, big change, and result. Dragon Ball starts with Goku as a child, and his search for the seven balls begins his adventure. Z shows him as an adult and father, while battles for Earth become central.

GT changes Goku into a child again and sends him into space. It is a separate side continuation. Super continues the main line after Buu and introduces gods and other universes. Therefore, the order is not simply Dragon Ball, Z, GT, and then Super. Both GT and Super follow Z, but they take different story roads.
