Before you begin

Warm-up

  1. What are you going to do this weekend?

  2. What do you say when a friend has a heavy bag?

  3. Do you make plans early or decide at the last minute?

Reading

Part 1: Be Going To for Plans

It is Friday. Ken decided last week to play volleyball on Saturday. Mia asks about his plan.

We use be going to for a plan we made before speaking. Use am, is or are + going to + a base verb. A base verb is the simple verb, like play, watch or buy.

A pronoun takes the place of a name, like he for Ken. Use am with I. Use is with he, she and it. Use are with you, we and they. Keep going to and the base verb the same.

For a question, put am, is or are before the person. For a negative, put not after am, is or are.

Ken: I am going to play volleyball tomorrow.

Mia: Are you going to play volleyball tomorrow?

Ken: Yes, I am.

A no answer is No, I'm not. You changes to I when you answer about yourself.

The negative changes the meaning: I am not going to play volleyball tomorrow.

Change the person:

Is Ken going to play? Yes, he is. No, he isn't.

Is Mia going to play? Yes, she is. No, she isn't.

Are Ken and Mia going to play? Yes, they are. No, they aren't.

No, he's not and No, she's not are also correct. So is No, they're not. Use the long form after yes: Yes, I am, not Yes, I'm.

For more information, put a question word before am, is or are.

What are you going to do tomorrow?

I'm going to play volleyball.

What asks about the activity; the answer gives the plan.

Do not leave out be: I going to play is wrong. Do not use do or does in these questions. Keep the base verb: She is going to play, not She is going to plays.

    Reading

    Part 2: Will for Decisions, Offers and Promises

    At the game, Ken says: Oh no, I forgot my water!

    Mia says: I'll give you some of mine. She decides now.

    Use will for a decision made now, an offer or a promise. An offer is when we say we can help someone. A promise is when we say we will really do something.

    The form is will + a base verb. Will stays the same for every person. I will help becomes She will help.

    For a question, put will before the person. For a negative, use will not or won't.

    Statement: You will bring water.

    Question: Will you bring water?

    Short answers: Yes, I will. No, I won't. No, I will not.

    Negative: I will not bring water. I won't bring water.

    Again, you changes to I when you answer about yourself.

    Change the person:

    Will Ken help? Yes, he will. No, he won't.

    Will Mia help? Yes, she will. No, she won't.

    Will Ken and Mia help? Yes, they will. No, they won't.

    For more information, put a question word before will.

    When will you bring the water?

    I'll bring it after the game.

    When asks about time.

    Your bag looks heavy. I'll carry it for you. This is an offer.

    I'll call you after the game. This is a promise.

    Do not add -s or to after will. She will helps and She will to help are wrong. Do you will help? is also wrong. Use Will you help?

    Compare these answers when Mia says: We need more balls for practice.

    Ken: I know. I'm going to bring two from home.

    Ken: Oh, OK. I'll bring two from home.

    The first answer shows an earlier plan. The second shows a decision now. The forms often overlap when we talk about the future.

    A prediction is what we think will happen. Our team will win and Our team is going to win can both be correct. With a sign now, we often choose going to. Look at those dark clouds. It's going to rain.

      Part 3: Spelling the Short Forms I'll, We'll and Won't

      In speaking and messages to friends, we often use short forms. An apostrophe shows where letters are missing. It is a small mark at the top of the letters.

      I will becomes I'll, and we will becomes we'll. Each apostrophe takes the place of wi.

      Will not becomes won't. This spelling is special: will changes to wo, and not becomes n't. The apostrophe takes the place of the o in not. Do not write willn't.

      I'll see you on Saturday.

      We'll bring the ball.

      I won't be late.

      They'll wait for us at the door.

      Keep the apostrophe: well and ill are different words. Ill means sick.

      Use the long form after yes: Yes, I will, not Yes, I'll. After no, won't is correct: No, I won't.

        Speaking

        Work with a partner. Ask about weekend plans with be going to. Give short answers, then ask what or when for more information. Next, give your partner a problem. Your partner makes an offer with will. Ask a will question and answer it. Finish with a promise using won't. Change roles.

        We can now ask about the future and give clear answers.

        1. What is one plan you made before today?

        2. How does your question change when you ask about one friend?

        3. Which short form is hardest for you to spell?
