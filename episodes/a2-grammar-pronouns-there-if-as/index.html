Introduction

Welcome! This lesson has four parts. You will learn how to:

  • use words like him, her, and them after verbs
  • say what is in a place with there is and there are
  • talk about conditions with if
  • say that two things are the same with as … as

Each part has a short explanation with examples, then a test. Read the examples slowly and try saying them out loud.

Before you begin

Warm-up

  1. Who do you often talk to after school? What do you talk about?

  2. What things are in your room right now?

  3. What will you do this weekend if the weather is nice?

  4. Think of a friend or family member. Are you the same height as that person?

Reading

Part 1: Object Pronouns

A pronoun is a short word that takes the place of a noun. We use subject pronouns before the verb, and object pronouns after the verb.

Subject (before the verb)Object (after the verb)
Ime
youyou
hehim
sheher
itit
weus
theythem

Rule 1: Use an object pronoun after a verb.

  • I know him. (not I know he.)
  • My mother loves me.
  • These shoes are nice. I want them.

Rule 2: Use an object pronoun after a preposition (with, for, to, at, and others).

  • I often play with her.
  • This present is for you.
  • Please listen to us.

Tip: First think about who the person is. One boy or man is him. One girl or woman is her. More than one person or thing is them. One thing is it.

  • My uncle is funny. I like him.
  • My aunt lives in a big city. I visit her every summer.
  • I have a new bike. I ride it to school.

    Reading

    Part 2: There Is and There Are

    We use there is and there are to say that something is in a place.

    Rule 1: Use "there is" with one thing. Use "there are" with two or more things.

    • There is a cat on the sofa.
    • There are two cats on the sofa.
    • There's a clock on the wall. (There's = There is)

    Rule 2: Negative sentences use "there isn't" and "there aren't." We often use any in negative sentences.

    • There isn't a TV in my room.
    • There aren't any eggs in the fridge.

    Rule 3: For questions, put "is" or "are" before "there."

    • Is there a station near here? — Yes, there is. / No, there isn't.
    • Are there any books in your bag? — Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.

    Rule 4: To ask about a number, use "How many + plural noun + are there …?"

    • How many chairs are there in the room? — There are six chairs.

    Tip: For short answers, use there, not it or they.

    • Is there a bus stop on this street? — Yes, there is. (not Yes, it is.)

      Reading

      Part 3: If Sentences

      We use if to talk about a condition. A condition is something that may happen. The other part of the sentence tells the result.

      • If it is sunny, we will go to the beach.
      • Condition: it is sunny
      • Result: we will go to the beach

      Rule 1: In the "if" part, use the present tense, even when you talk about the future.

      • If it rains tomorrow, I will stay home. (not If it will rain tomorrow …)
      • If you are free, let's play a game.
      • If she gets up early, she will catch the first train.

      Rule 2: The other part often uses "will," "can," or a request like "please" or "let's."

      • If you finish your homework, you can watch TV.
      • If you have a question, please ask me.

      Rule 3: The "if" part can come first or second. When it comes first, put a comma after it. When it comes second, you don't need a comma.

      • If you are tired, you can sleep.
      • You can sleep if you are tired.

        Reading

        Part 4: As … As

        We use as + adjective + as to say that two people or things are the same in some way.

        Rule 1: Use the basic form of the adjective between "as" and "as." Don't use the "-er" or "-est" form.

        • My bag is as heavy as your bag. (not as heavier as)
        • This room is as big as that room.
        • Today is as cold as yesterday.

        Rule 2: You can also use the basic form of an adverb.

        • My brother runs as fast as my father.
        • I can sing as well as my sister.

        Rule 3: "Not as … as" means "less than." It shows that two things are not the same.

        • My dog is not as big as your dog. (Your dog is bigger.)
        • This test is not as difficult as the last test. (The last test was more difficult.)

        Tip: Always use as two times. Don't use than with as … as.

        • She is as tall as her mother. (not as tall than)
