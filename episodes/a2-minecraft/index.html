Before you begin

Warm-up

  1. Have you ever played Minecraft, or watched someone play it? What did you notice?

  2. Do you like games with clear goals, or games where you can do anything?

  3. Why do you think some games stay popular for many years?

Reading

Part 1: A Game Without a Goal

Most video games give you a clear goal. In a racing game, you must win the race. Minecraft is different. Nobody tells you what to do. You can build a house, explore a valley, or just walk around.

Minecraft gives players freedom. There are no goals that you must reach. You choose how to play. Some players build big structures from blocks. Others explore the world. Some players craft things, which means they make new items from simple materials. In survival play, you must also stay alive. In creative play, you can simply build what you want.

This is like a box of toy bricks. The box does not tell you what to make. The bricks only give you the pieces. In the same way, Minecraft gives you a world and lets you choose. So the game is not one activity. It is many possible activities in one place.

1. What makes Minecraft different from a racing game?

Sample answer

A racing game has a clear goal, but Minecraft has no required goals. Players choose to build, explore, craft, survive or play creatively.

Think one step further

Would you enjoy a game with no goal, or would you feel lost? Why?

Reading

Part 2: One Man and Someone Else's Idea

Many people think a big company planned Minecraft. In fact, it began with one man. Markus Persson created the first Minecraft, and he wrote it in Java, a computer language.

Persson did not start from nothing. In April 2009, another game called Infiniminer came out. It gave Persson ideas for his own early project, called RubyDung. Infiniminer influenced three important things: the blocky look, the first-person view, and the building with blocks. In a first-person view, you see the world through the eyes of your character.

The exact early dates are not completely certain. But the usual story is this: in May 2009, the game began as Cave Game. In December 2009, it entered a stage called Indev. Then, in June 2010, it entered the Alpha stage.

So Minecraft did not appear as a finished product. It began as an experiment. It borrowed good ideas from another game and slowly became something new.

Sample answer

Infiniminer, released in April 2009, inspired his project RubyDung. It influenced the blocky style, the first-person view and the block-building play.

Think one step further

Reading

Part 3: From a Creator to a Team

Minecraft grew step by step, in public. Each stage, from Cave Game to Indev to Alpha, was a new and better version. Then, in 2011, the full game came out.

After the full release, something important changed. On 1 December 2011, Persson stepped down as lead developer. He gave control to Jens Bergensten. This means the game did not depend on its creator anymore. A team could now carry it forward.

The game kept growing after that. In 2012, adventure mode and command blocks arrived. With these tools, players could create their own maps with their own rules and challenges, and share them with others. So players were not only playing the game. They were also making new games inside it.

This matters. A game that only one person controls can stop when that person leaves. Minecraft moved from one creator to a team, and also gave players tools to create. That helped it keep changing.

Reading

Part 4: Microsoft Buys a Success

Now we come back to the common idea that Microsoft made Minecraft. It did not. Microsoft bought Minecraft much later.

In November 2014, Microsoft bought Mojang, the company behind the game. It also bought the rights to Minecraft as an idea and a product. The price was 2.5 billion US dollars.

Look at the timeline. Persson started the game in 2009. The full game came out in 2011. Microsoft bought it in 2014. So Microsoft did not create the success. It paid for a game that was already successful. That is why the price was so high. A company does not pay billions for an untested idea. It pays for something that people already love.

Check: Part 4: Microsoft Buys a Success

1. Did Microsoft create Minecraft? Explain with dates.

Sample answer

No. Markus Persson created it, starting in 2009, and the full game came out in 2011. Microsoft bought Mojang and Minecraft in November 2014 for 2.5 billion US dollars.

Think one step further

Why do you think a big company would rather buy a successful game than make a new one?

Reading

Part 5: Best-Selling: What We Know and What We Guess

Minecraft has sold over 400 million copies. It is the best-selling video game ever. Even before the full release in 2011, it had sold more than four million copies.

The harder question is why. Some things certainly helped the game become famous. People shared it on social media. Fans made funny copies of it. There were other versions of the game, products to buy, and fan meetings called Minecon. All of these spread the name of the game.

But be careful here. Many people say Minecraft sold so well because of its freedom. That is a reasonable idea, because the game has no required goals and lets you build, explore and survive. But nobody has proved it. It is one possible explanation.

So we need to separate two things. We know the sales number, because people counted the copies. We do not know all the reasons for the sales. When someone gives a reason, we should ask: can they show it, or are they guessing?

What you can do now

Final Reflection

At the start, you might have thought Microsoft built Minecraft as a big planned hit. In fact, it began with one man in 2009, grew in public stages, passed to a team in 2011, and was bought by Microsoft only in 2014. Its sales are a clear fact, but the reasons for its success are still partly a guess.

  1. Which part of Minecraft's story surprised you most, and why?

  2. When you hear why something became popular, how can you tell if it is a fact or a guess?
