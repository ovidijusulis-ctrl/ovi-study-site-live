Introduction

Pastries and Light Food at a Cafe

Cafes sell more than drinks. Many also sell pastries and light food such as croissants, muffins, sandwiches, salads, and toasties.

Knowing a few food words helps you order clearly and feel ready. You will read about cafe food options and how to order them, then practise in three short conversations.

Before you begin

Warm-Up Questions

  1. Do you like sweet food or savoury food at a cafe?

    Need a starting line?

    Cafes often sell both sweet pastries and savoury sandwiches.

  2. What light food do you usually buy with a drink?

    Need a starting line?

    Many people order food and a drink together.

  3. Can you name one pastry you know?

    Need a starting line?

    Pastries are baked goods that people often eat in the morning.

Reading

Cafe Pastries and Light Food

Many cafes keep a glass case of pastries near the counter. A butter croissant is flaky and light. A blueberry muffin is soft and sweet. Some places also sell a cheese danish or a cinnamon roll. These cafe pastries are popular in the morning with tea or coffee.

Light food is good when you want more than a snack but not a big meal. A chicken sandwich is easy to eat with your hands. A cheese toastie is a hot sandwich with melted cheese. A garden salad is cooler and lighter. At places like Pret A Manger, people often grab a sandwich or a salad to eat for lunch. You can ask what is fresh today, or point to the item you want in the case.

Check: Cafe Food Options

1. Name two pastries and two light food items you might find at a cafe.

Sample answer

Pastries are usually sweet or flaky baked goods. Light food is a small meal such as a sandwich or salad.

Think one step further

Which of these would you choose for lunch?

Useful language

Useful Phrases: Talking About Food

What pastries do you have today?
You ask which baked items are available now.What pastries do you have today? I want a sweet pastry.
Is the muffin fresh?
You ask if the muffin was baked recently.Is the muffin fresh? I prefer soft ones.
I would like a cheese toastie, please.
A polite way to order a hot cheese sandwich.I would like a cheese toastie, please. For here.
Can I see the salad options?
You ask the barista to show or list the salads.Can I see the salad options? I want a light meal.

Reading

How to Order Food at a Cafe

Ordering food at a cafe follows a simple pattern. The barista greets you. You say the food item clearly. Then the barista may ask if you want it heated, or ask for here or to go.

Keep the order short. Say the full dish name as one unit, such as butter croissant or garden salad. If you want a change, add it after the item: no butter, extra cheese, or no onion. After that, the barista tells you the price. You pay, then wait for your name or for the food at the counter.

You can order food alone, or add a simple drink if you want. Practise the food words clearly before you add extras.

What is one change you might ask for with a sandwich?

Useful language

Useful Phrases: Ordering Food

Can you heat that up, please?
You ask the barista to warm the food.A cheese toastie, please. Can you heat that up, please?
For here or to go?
The barista asks if you will eat inside or take the food away.For here or to go? To go, please.
No onion, please.
You ask the barista to leave onion out of the food.A chicken sandwich, please. No onion, please.
How much is that?
You ask the total price.How much is that? I will pay by card.

Reading

Changes, Extras, and Paying

After you name the food, you can ask for a small change. Say no onion, extra cheese, or no butter. If the food is cold, you can ask the barista to heat it up. These short lines keep the order clear.

When the food is ready, the barista may call your name or bring it to the counter. You can pay by card or with cash. If you also want a drink, say the food first, then the drink. Keep each part short so the barista can repeat the order back to you.

Roleplay

Roleplay: Pastry Order

Maya

Good morning! What can I get for you today?

You

Good morning. What pastries do you have today?

Maya

We have butter croissants and blueberry muffins. Both are fresh this morning.

You

Is the muffin soft?

Maya

Yes. The blueberry muffin is soft and sweet.

You

I would like a blueberry muffin, please.

Maya

Sure. For here or to go?

You

For here, please. How much is that?

Maya

That is three fifty. I will bring it over in a moment.

Vocabulary

Useful Topic Vocabulary

croissant
A flaky, curved pastry often eaten in the morning.I ordered a butter croissant with my tea.
muffin
A soft, sweet cake baked in a small round shape.The blueberry muffin was still warm.
toastie
A hot toasted sandwich, often with melted cheese.A cheese toastie is perfect on a cold day.
barista
A person who works at a cafe and takes your order.The barista asked if I wanted the sandwich heated.
to go
Food or drink that you take away instead of eating in the cafe.For here or to go? To go, please.
