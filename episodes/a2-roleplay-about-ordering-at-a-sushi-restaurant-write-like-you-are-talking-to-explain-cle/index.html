Before you begin

Your choices

  1. What food would you like in your sushi?

  2. What do you ask when you do not know a dish?

  3. What would you like to drink with your food?

Reading

Start with the available choices

At a sushi counter, Mira is the server. Her menu has salmon, tuna, shrimp, egg, and a cucumber roll.

A customer wants lunch but has not chosen a dish. The customer first asks what is available.

Mira says that every item on this menu is available today. The customer then asks about the cucumber roll.

In this example, it has cucumber and rice inside seaweed, with no fish. That answer helps the customer choose food they want.

The customer orders the roll instead of guessing from its name. A short question about a dish can make an order clear before the server starts preparing it.

Check your understanding

Choose with clear information

1. The customer wants a dish without fish. How does asking about the cucumber roll help?

Sample answer

The answer describes this restaurant's dish, so the customer does not need to guess.

Useful language

Ask before choosing

What is available today?
You ask which dishes you can order now.What is available today? I want something simple.
What is in the cucumber roll?
You ask what is inside one dish.What is in the cucumber roll? I want to know before I order.
Does it have fish?
You ask a more exact question about one dish.Does it have fish? I want a dish without fish.
I'd like a cucumber roll, please.
You give your choice after the server answers.I'd like a cucumber roll, please. For here.

Reading

Say the item and the amount

In another order, Ken is the sushi chef. His menu offers salmon, tuna, shrimp, and egg sushi by the piece.

The cucumber roll comes as one whole roll, cut into smaller pieces. A customer asks for two pieces of salmon and one cucumber roll.

Ken repeats both parts of the order. This matters because two pieces and two rolls are different amounts.

The customer listens and confirms the order. Then the customer adds one piece of egg sushi.

Ken repeats the complete order again before preparing it. Naming the food and the amount together helps both people understand exactly what the customer wants to eat.

Check your understanding

Make the amount clear

1. Why is “two pieces of salmon and one cucumber roll” clearer than “salmon and cucumber”?

Sample answer

An item name alone does not give the amount.

Useful language

Order and confirm

Two pieces of salmon, please.
You name the item and the amount.Two pieces of salmon, please.
One cucumber roll, please.
You order a roll instead of pieces.One cucumber roll, please.
And one piece of egg sushi, please.
You add another item.And one piece of egg sushi, please.
Yes, that's right.
You confirm the order.Yes, that's right.
One roll, not two, please.
You correct a mistake politely.One roll, not two, please.

Reading

Ask for extras and the bill

Back at Mira's counter, a customer has chosen shrimp sushi. This order comes with soy sauce, but no ginger or wasabi.

The customer wants ginger and asks for it separately. Mira brings a small dish of ginger beside the sushi.

The customer does not want wasabi, so Mira leaves it out. Tea is available in this situation, and the customer orders a cup.

After eating, the customer asks for the bill. Mira brings a bill that lists the sushi and tea.

The customer checks that both items match the order. In this example, card payment is accepted, and the customer pays by card before leaving.

Clear requests settle the extras, and checking the bill settles the payment.

Roleplay

Ask and choose

You

Hello. What is available today?

Mira, server

We have salmon, tuna, shrimp, egg sushi, and cucumber rolls.

You

What is in the cucumber roll?

Mira, server

Cucumber and rice inside seaweed. There is no fish in it.

You

I'd like one cucumber roll, please.

Mira, server

One cucumber roll, with no fish. Is that right?

You

Yes, that's right. Thank you.

Mira, server

Thank you. Your order is one cucumber roll.

Scene

Build your order

Ordering several items: you are at a different counter with Ken, the sushi chef. Salmon and egg sushi are sold by the piece, while a cucumber roll is ordered as one whole roll cut into smaller pieces.

Your goal is to order two pieces of salmon, one cucumber roll, and one piece of egg sushi, then confirm the complete order.

Roleplay

Request and pay

Mira, server

Here is your shrimp sushi with soy sauce.

You

Could I have some ginger, please? No wasabi, please.

Mira, server

Of course. Here is the ginger in a separate dish, with no wasabi.

You

And a cup of tea, please.

Mira, server

Here is your tea. Enjoy your meal.

You

Thank you. I've finished now. Could I have the bill, please?

Mira, server

Here it is. It lists your shrimp sushi and tea.

You

Yes, that's right. Can I pay by card?

Mira, server

Yes. Please use the card reader here.

You

I've paid. Thank you.

Mira, server

Your payment is complete. Thank you.

Speak

Make your own complete order

A new order: you are speaking to a server at a sushi counter. The available choices are salmon, tuna, shrimp, egg sushi, and cucumber rolls; ginger, soy sauce, and tea are also available. Give your full order aloud, including each amount, one extra, and a drink. Then ask for the bill. Repeat your order once more as a clear confirmation.

What you can do now

Final Reflection

  1. A clear sushi order names the food and the amount. Requests for extras and the bill help complete the meal. What would you say to order your food, ask for an extra, and finish paying?

Useful Topic Vocabulary

available

something added to the main orderGinger is an extra in this situation.
