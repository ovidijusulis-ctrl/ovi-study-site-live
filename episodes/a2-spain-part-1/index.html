Reading

Part 1: People and Everyday Life

Most people in Spain live in flats, not houses. Their homes are often small, and the weather is often warm. So a lot of life happens outside.

People meet friends in cafés and in the plaza. The plaza is the big square in the middle of a town. It is like a big living room for everyone.

In summer, the afternoons are very hot. So people stay inside in the hottest hours. In the evening, the air gets cooler. So they go out for the paseo, a slow evening walk.

Families walk through the town and say hello to neighbours. The paseo is not for sport. It is a way to see your town and your friends.

Part 2: 1492, a Big Year in History

Before 1492, Spain was not one country. It had several kingdoms. A kingdom is a country with a king or a queen. The biggest were Castile and Aragon. In the south was Granada, the last kingdom with Muslim rulers.

Isabella was the future queen of Castile, and Ferdinand was the future king of Aragon. They married, so their kingdoms worked together.

Isabella and Ferdinand are called the Catholic Monarchs. Monarch is another word for king or queen. In 1492, they took Granada. Now they ruled almost all of Spain.

In the same year, they paid for the ships of a sailor called Columbus. He sailed west, and on 12 October 1492, he arrived in America. That is why 12 October is the national day of Spain.

Part 3: Regions and Languages

Spain has 17 regions, called autonomous communities. Each one has its own government. It decides many things for itself, like schools and hospitals.

Why? Long ago, Spain had several kingdoms. That is why the regions are still different today. Some even have their own language.

Spanish is spoken all over Spain. People there often call it castellano, because it first came from Castile. Around Barcelona, in the north-east, people also speak Catalan. In the Basque Country, in the north, people also speak Basque.

Most languages belong to a language family. It is a group of languages that grew from one old language. For example, Spanish and Catalan both grew from Latin.

But Basque is not in the same family as any other language. So people who speak only Spanish cannot understand it.

Part 4: Famous Places

In Granada, you can see the Alhambra. It was the palace of the Muslim kings there. The name is Arabic for the red one. Why? The walls are made of red earth.

In Barcelona, there is the Sagrada Família, a very big church by Gaudí. Building started in 1882, and it is still not finished.

Why is it so slow? The church gets no money from the government. Tickets and gifts from visitors pay for the work. So it can only grow when there is money.

Part 5: Food and Meals

Lunch is the biggest meal of the day, and dinner comes late. These are the usual times:

  • Lunch: about 2 pm
  • Dinner: 9 pm or later

Why so late? One reason is the clock. In 1940, the dictator Franco moved the clocks one hour forward. A dictator is a ruler with all the power. The clocks never went back.

So Spain's clocks are ahead of the sun. In winter, they are about one hour ahead. So dinner at 9 pm is like 8 pm by the sun.

Lunch is also long. Why? It is the biggest meal, so people eat it slowly.

After the meal, people stay at the table and talk. There is a word for this time: sobremesa, or over the table.

A famous lunch dish is paella, from Valencia. It is rice cooked in a wide, flat pan. In Valencia, people call this pan a paella. So the dish has the same name as the pan.

Part 6: The Seville Fair and Flamenco

Every spring, Seville has its big fair. It started as a market for buying and selling farm animals. Today it is like a small town of colourful tents along long streets.

Each tent is called a caseta. Most of them belong to families, groups of friends, clubs or companies. So you usually go in only when the owners invite you. Inside, people eat, drink and dance sevillanas, a happy dance for couples.

Seville is in Andalusia, the home of flamenco. Flamenco is singing, guitar music and dance together. The dancer often hits the floor hard with the shoes. So the shoes make music too. The Romani people of Andalusia helped to create flamenco.

Sources and fact review (16)
  1. Housing in Europe 2024 (Eurostat)

    most people in Spain live in flats (about two thirds, the highest share in the EU)

  2. The Paseo in Spain

    the paseo as a social evening walk after the heat of the day

  3. Catholic Monarchs (Wikipedia)

    marriage of Isabella of Castile and Ferdinand of Aragon; Granada taken in 1492; they paid for Columbus's ships; landing on 12 October 1492

  4. The conquest of Granada (Britannica)

    Granada was the last kingdom with Muslim rulers

  5. Fiesta Nacional de España (Wikipedia)

    12 October as Spain's national day, remembering Columbus's arrival

  6. Autonomous communities of Spain (Wikipedia)

    17 autonomous communities with their own governments, which run things like education and health

  7. Catalan language (Wikipedia)

    Catalan spoken in north-east Spain; Catalan, like Spanish, is a Romance language that grew from Latin

  8. Basque language (Wikipedia)

    Basque is not related to any other known language (a language isolate)

  9. Alhambra (Wikipedia)

    palace of Granada's Muslim rulers; name from Arabic for the red one; red earth walls

  10. Sagrada Família (Wikipedia)

    by Gaudí; building started 1882; paid for by tickets and gifts; still not finished

  11. Time in Spain (Wikipedia)

    Mainland Spain uses Central European Time (UTC+1, UTC+2 in summer) since 1940; before that most of Spain used GMT

  12. Why Spain is still in the wrong time zone because of Hitler (The Local)

    Franco moved the clocks one hour forward in 1940, and Spain is still in that time zone, about an hour ahead of the sun; lunch about 2 pm and dinner about 9 pm or later

  13. Why Do Shops in Spain Close in the Afternoon? (Spain Handbook)

    lunch is the largest meal of the day and a long one

  14. Paella (Wikipedia)

    from Valencia; wide flat pan; paella is the Valencian word for the pan

  15. Seville Fair (Wikipedia)

    held in spring; started as a livestock fair; streets of casetas owned by families, friends, clubs and companies; sevillanas

  16. Flamenco (UNESCO)

    flamenco joins song, dance and music; from Andalusia; important role of the Gitano (Romani) community
