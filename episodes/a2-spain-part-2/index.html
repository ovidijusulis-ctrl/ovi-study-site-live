Part 1: Two Kisses to Say Hello

When people in Spain meet, they often give dos besos. Dos besos is Spanish for two kisses, one on each cheek. It is not a real kiss. The cheeks just touch. People make a small kiss sound in the air. Women do this with women, and men do it with women. People do it with friends and family. They also do it with someone new, like a friend of a friend.

Two men usually shake hands. If they are close friends or family, they often hug. At work, more people now shake hands, especially at a first meeting. For Spanish people, the two kisses are a normal, warm hello, like a smile. So a Spanish person may feel a little hurt if a visitor steps back.

Part 2: Two Family Names

Most people in Spain have one first name and two family names. The first family name usually comes from the father. The second family name comes from the mother. So one name shows both sides of the family. The famous painter Pablo Picasso shows this. His two family names were Ruiz and Picasso. Ruiz was his father's family name, and Picasso was his mother's. Later, he used only his mother's name.

When women in Spain marry, they do not change their names. They keep the family names from their parents all their lives. Today, parents can choose which family name comes first. But all the children in one family must have the same order. So brothers and sisters always have the same two family names.

Part 3: Las Fallas, a Festival of Fire

Every March, the city of Valencia has a big festival called Las Fallas. Each part of the city makes a falla. A falla is a very big group of many figures. Each figure in a falla is a ninot. Fallas are made of wood and paper, and some are as tall as a building. Many are funny, because they make jokes about famous people and the news.

On the night of 19 March, people burn almost all of them. This fire night is called la Cremà. Why burn something so beautiful? The idea comes from carpenters, people who make things from wood. In winter, they used old wood to hold candles, so they could work at night. At the end of winter, the days got longer, so they burned the old wood. They did it on 19 March, the day of Saint Joseph. Saint Joseph also worked with wood, so he was special to them. Every year, visitors vote for their favourite ninot. The winners are saved from the fire and go to a museum.

Part 4: The Canary Islands

The Canary Islands are seven big islands in the Atlantic Ocean. They are part of Spain, but only about 100 kilometres from Africa. The islands were made by volcanoes. The volcano Teide is on Tenerife, the biggest island. It is the highest mountain in Spain.

People call them the islands of eternal spring, a spring that never ends. The weather is warm all year, and it does not change much. Winters are warm, because the islands are far south. In summer, the sea is cool and the wind blows on most days. So winter and summer are not very different.

Part 5: The Camino de Santiago

Camino de Santiago is Spanish for the Way of Saint James. It is a long walk to the city of Santiago de Compostela. The city is in the north-west of Spain. Saint James was one of the first people to follow Jesus. People believe he is buried in the big church of the city. So for more than a thousand years, pilgrims have walked there. Pilgrims are people who travel to a special church or place. The most popular way starts in France and is about 800 kilometres long.

People on the Camino carry a pilgrim passport. In each town where they sleep, they get a stamp in it. The stamps show where they walked. At the end, pilgrims can get the Compostela. It is a special paper with their name on it. To get it, pilgrims must walk at least the last 100 kilometres. On this part, they need at least two stamps every day. That is why many people start in Sarria, just over 100 kilometres from Santiago.

Roleplay

Conversation: Sunday Lunch with a Spanish Family

Lucía

Come in! This is my mum, Carmen, and this is my dad, Javier.

You

Hello, nice to meet you.

Carmen

Nice to meet you too! Two kisses, the Spanish way.

You

In Japan, we usually bow. But I like this!

Javier

Welcome to our home. Please sit down.

You

Thank you. Lucía, what is your full name?

Lucía

It's Lucía Martínez García. Martínez comes from my dad, and García comes from my mum.

You

Carmen, did you change your family names when you married?

Carmen

No. Women in Spain keep their names. I am still Carmen García López.

Javier

Lunch is ready. It's Sunday, so we're having paella.

You

Do you eat paella every Sunday?

Carmen

Many Sundays, yes. Here in Valencia, paella is a family lunch. We never eat it at night.

You

Why not at night?

Carmen

Lunch is our big meal of the day. And paella takes a long time to cook. At night, we eat something small and light, so we sleep well.

You

That was delicious. Should I go now?

Lucía

No, stay! Now it's the sobremesa. We sit and talk, often for hours.

You

What are your plans for Las Fallas?

Javier

On 19 March, we go to la Cremà. It's Saint Joseph's day, so it's also Father's Day in Spain.

You

Can I come with you?

Lucía

Of course! Meet us at the City Hall Square at half past nine.

You

Great. See you on the 19th!

Conversation: The Night of Fire in Valencia

You

Hi, Lucía! Happy Father's Day, Javier!

Javier

Thank you!

You

What time does la Cremà start?

Lucía

It starts with the children's fallas at eight. At ten, the big fallas all over the city burn.

You

And this one?

Lucía

This is the City Hall falla. It burns last, at eleven.

You

Will they burn all the ninots?

Javier

Almost all. Visitors vote for their favourite ninot, and the winners go to the museum.

You

But it's so beautiful. Why do you burn it?

Javier

It's like the carpenters long ago. At the end of winter, we burn the old things.

Lucía

And when the fire ends, this year's Fallas end. Then next year's Fallas begin.

You

So we wait here until eleven?

Lucía

Yes. Then we watch it burn!

Conversation: Starting the Camino in Sarria

You

Hello! Do you have a bed for tonight?

Manuel

Yes, we do. Are you walking to Santiago?

You

Yes, I'm starting tomorrow. How much is a bed for one night?

Manuel

It's 10 euros. Pilgrims can stay here for one night only. Do you have a pilgrim passport?

You

Not yet. Where can I get one?

Manuel

You can buy one here. It's 2 euros.

You

Great. Can I have a stamp, please?

Manuel

Here you are, your first stamp. From here, you need two stamps every day.

You

How far is it to Santiago?

Manuel

About 115 kilometres. People usually walk it in five days.

You

Where do people sleep tomorrow night?

Manuel

Many stop in Portomarín. It's 22 kilometres from here.

You

And what do I do in Santiago?

Manuel

Go to the Pilgrim Office with your pilgrim passport. They give you the Compostela, and it's free.

You

Thank you so much!

Sources and fact review (16)
  1. The most common mistakes foreigners make when greeting people in Spain (The Local)

    two kisses, cheeks touch, men shake hands or hug, handshakes at work, people may feel hurt if you pull back

  2. Spanish naming customs (Wikipedia)

    father's and mother's family names, women keep their names, parents choose the order, same order for all children

  3. Spain changes administrative guidelines on surname order (American Name Society)

    parents now choose which family name comes first

  4. What is Picasso's full name? (Britannica)

    Ruiz from his father, Picasso from his mother, he later used only Picasso

  5. Falles (Wikipedia)

    fallas made of wood and paper, building-sized, jokes about famous people and news, la Cremà on 19 March, carpenters' candle wood burned on Saint Joseph's day, ninots saved by vote and kept in a museum

  6. La Cremà (Visit Valencia)

    children's fallas burn at 8 pm, all big fallas at 10 pm, the City Hall Square falla at 11 pm, 19 March is Saint Joseph's day and Father's Day, the end of one year's Fallas and the start of the next

  7. Spanish word of the day: sobremesa (The Local)

    sobremesa means over the table, time after a meal spent talking with family or friends

  8. Why paella is eaten for lunch and not dinner in Spain (The Local)

    in Valencia paella is cooked at home for the family on Sundays, eaten at lunch and not at night; lunch is the big meal of the day, a rice dish for several people takes time to make, and Spaniards eat something lighter before bed; the talk after the meal can last for hours

  9. Canary Islands (Wikipedia)

    seven main islands, about 100 km from Africa, volcanic islands, Teide the highest mountain in Spain, climate moderated by the sea and the trade winds

  10. An eternal spring (Hello Canary Islands)

    the name islands of eternal spring, mild weather all year

  11. Camino de Santiago (Wikipedia)

    Saint James's tomb in the cathedral, more than a thousand years of pilgrims, French Way from France about 800 km, pilgrim passport and stamps, Sarria

  12. The pilgrim's credential (Camino de Santiago, Xunta de Galicia)

    Compostela needs at least the last 100 km on foot and at least two stamps a day on that part, Sarria as the usual walking start on the French Way

  13. Note from the Chapter on Credenciales (Pilgrim's Reception Office, Santiago)

    the pilgrim credential costs no more than 2 euros

  14. Pilgrim credentials: everything you need to know (Cicerone)

    the Pilgrim Office gives the Compostela for free, credentials can be picked up at albergues on the Camino

  15. The public network of pilgrim hostels of the Xunta de Galicia (Albergues Camino Santiago)

    public pilgrim hostels cost 10 euros a night, one night only

  16. How the stages of the Camino from Sarria are organised (Santiago Ways)

    about 115 km from Sarria to Santiago in five stages, first stage Sarria to Portomarín 22 km
