Reading

Part 1: People and everyday life

Spain is in the south-west of Europe. Many people in Spain live in big cities. Madrid is the capital and the biggest city. Barcelona is another very big city. Other people live on islands in the sea. So daily life can look very different from place to place.

Work days can feel different too. In the past, many shops and businesses closed in the middle of the day. They often closed for two or three hours. This gave people time for a long lunch at home. Family and friends are an important part of the day.

Reading

Part 2: A little history

A long time ago, Muslim rulers governed a big part of Spain. People call these lands Al-Andalus. Al-Andalus lasted from 711 to 1492. That is almost 800 years.

The city of Granada was the last Muslim kingdom. In 1492, it was taken by a Christian queen and king. The Alhambra in Granada is from the time of Al-Andalus. It is an old palace and a strong building that protected the city.

Today, Spain is very different. People choose their government by voting. The country also has a king. Spain is part of the European Union.

Part 3: Regions and languages

Spain has 17 regions with their own governments. Each region makes some of its own decisions. Andalusia is in the south. Catalonia is in the north-east.

Everyone in Spain learns Spanish. It is the language for the whole country. But some regions have a second official language. People speak Catalan around Barcelona. People speak Galician in the north-west. People speak Basque in the north. Basque is very different from Spanish. It is not related to most other languages in Europe.

Part 4: Famous places

Let’s connect three cities with three famous places. In Barcelona, the Sagrada Familia is a Catholic church designed by Antoni Gaudi. It is the tallest church in the world, and parts of it are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

In Granada, Andalusia, the Alhambra brings us back to Al-Andalus. This old palace and fortress is a famous example of Islamic building design.

In central Madrid, the Museo del Prado is Spain’s main national art museum. It opened in 1819 and has many paintings by Francisco Goya and Diego Velazquez. These places offer different ways to explore Spain: through a church, a palace, or art.

Part 5: Food and meals

Lunch is usually Spain’s large main meal, starting around 2:00 to 2:30 in the afternoon. A small snack, called merienda, may come around 6:00. Dinner starts late, from about 8:30. After a meal, people may stay at the table to relax and talk. This time together is called sobremesa.

Food also connects with regions. Paella is a Valencian rice dish from the east coast, cooked in a wide, flat pan. Gazpacho is an Andalusian cold vegetable soup, often made with tomato and eaten in summer. Tortilla is a thick omelette with eggs and potatoes, often with onion. Tapas are small snacks that can be hot or cold. Several tapas together can make a full meal.

Part 6: The Seville Fair and flamenco

The Seville Fair, also called Feria de Abril, is an Andalusian fair in Seville. It usually begins two weeks after Holy Week, the week before Easter. The fair has rows of decorated tents called casetas. These belong to families, groups of friends, clubs, and other groups. From about nine at night until early morning, people dance sevillanas, a folk dance danced in pairs.

Southern Spain is also the home of flamenco. Flamenco is an Andalusian art of music, song, and dance. The Romani people of Andalusia helped create it. Like Valencian paella, flamenco has a local home, even though people know it far beyond that region.
