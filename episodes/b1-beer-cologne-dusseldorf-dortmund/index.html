Introduction

Imagine sitting down in a busy Cologne brewery pub. Before you even order, a waiter puts a small glass of beer in front of you.

In North Rhine-Westphalia, beer is a local identity. Before your trip, you will learn what Kölsch, Altbier and Dortmunder Export are, and where they came from. You will also learn how to drink them like a local, and why Cologne and Düsseldorf love to tease each other.

Before you begin

  2. Do you think the glass or the place can change how a drink tastes to you?

  3. Do you know any two cities that have a friendly rivalry?

Reading

Part 1: Kölsch, a Beer With Rules and Rituals

Kölsch is a pale, clear and light beer from Cologne. It is top-fermented, which means the yeast works at warmer temperatures. That method gives the beer a fresh, slightly fruity taste. Brewing has a long history in the city, and Cologne's brewers' guild dates back to 1396.

Not every beer can call itself Kölsch. In 1986, Cologne's breweries signed the Kölsch Konvention, an agreement that defines what the beer is and where it can be brewed. Later, the European Union gave the name geographical protection. So Kölsch is like Champagne: the place is part of the name. The word is also the name of the local dialect, so people joke that it is the only language you can drink.

The drinking customs are just as important. Kölsch comes in a narrow 0.2-litre glass called a Stange. The waiter is called a Köbes, and he carries the glasses in a round tray called a Kranz. When your glass is empty, the Köbes usually replaces it without asking. He also marks each beer on your beer mat. To stop, you put the beer mat on top of your glass. Famous brewery pubs in the Old Town include Früh, founded in 1904, and Päffgen, founded in 1883.

Why such small glasses? The beer stays cold and fresh, and the quick refills keep the room lively.

Check: Part 1: Kölsch, a Beer With Rules and Rituals

1. How do you tell a Köbes in Cologne that you do not want another Kölsch?

Sample answer

You put your beer mat on top of your glass. Otherwise, the Köbes usually replaces your empty glass with a full one.

Think one step further

Would you enjoy automatic refills, or would you prefer to order each drink yourself? Why?

Reading

Part 2: Altbier and the Friendly Rivalry

In Düsseldorf, people drink Altbier. Many visitors think "Alt" means old beer, but that is a mistake. "Alt" refers to the old way of brewing, with top-fermenting yeast, the same basic method used for Kölsch. The result is very different, though. Altbier is dark copper or brown, with a stronger malt flavour and more bitterness.

Altbier is usually served in a small glass, often 0.25 litres. As in Cologne, the waiters are called Köbes. The heart of this culture is the Old Town, which locals call "the longest bar in the world" because it has so many pubs close together. Traditional brewery pubs there include Schumacher (1838), Füchschen (1848) and Uerige (1862). Each one brews its own Altbier.

This explains the rivalry. The two cities are only about 40 kilometres apart. Their beers use a similar method and have similar customs, but they look and taste different. Small differences between close neighbours often create the strongest feelings. The rivalry also appears in Carnival and football. Cologne is the bigger city, but Düsseldorf is the state capital. Locals often joke that you should never order Kölsch in Düsseldorf or Altbier in Cologne. Mostly, this is humour, not real hostility.

Check: Part 2: Altbier and the Friendly Rivalry

1. What does "Alt" in Altbier really mean?

Sample answer

It refers to the old brewing method with top-fermenting yeast. It does not mean that the beer itself is old.

Think one step further

Why do you think neighbours with similar traditions often compete more than very different places do?

Reading

Part 3: Dortmunder Export, a Beer for Workers

Dortmund's story is different. It is a city in the Ruhr, a region that became rich from coal mining and steel. In the second half of the 1800s, many people did hard, hot work there, and local breweries grew quickly. Dortmunder Export developed in this world. It is a bottom-fermented pale lager, a little stronger than many everyday beers, with more malt and less bitterness than a Pils. The name "Export" shows that it was made to be sold far beyond the city.

For a long time, Dortmund was one of the biggest brewing cities in Europe. Famous names include Dortmunder Actien-Brauerei (DAB), founded in 1868, and Dortmunder Union. You can still see this history in the Dortmunder U. This former Union brewery building has a large glowing "U" on its roof, and today it is a centre for art and culture.

As heavy industry declined and Pils became more popular, Export lost much of its importance. The Ruhr's last coal mine closed in 2018. So in Dortmund, you will not find a strict glass ritual like Cologne's. The beer's meaning is in its story: it was the drink of an industrial city at work.

Check: Part 3: Dortmunder Export, a Beer for Workers

1. Why does Dortmunder Export have a different story from Kölsch and Altbier?

Sample answer

It grew out of the Ruhr's industrial life of mining and steel, and it was made to be sold widely. Its story is about work and large-scale brewing rather than small-glass pub customs.

Think one step further

Which of the three cities' beer traditions sounds most interesting to you, and why?

What you can do now

Final Reflection

Each of these beers tells you something about its city. Cologne cares about protection and ritual, Düsseldorf about pride in its difference, and Dortmund about its working past. When you visit, look at the glass and the waiter as well as the beer, because they tell part of the story too.

  1. What could you learn about a city by visiting its traditional pubs rather than its museums?

  2. Would you try both Kölsch and Altbier and choose a favourite, or stay neutral in the rivalry?
