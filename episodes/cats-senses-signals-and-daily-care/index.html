Reading

How Cats Sense the World

Cats use their ears, eyes, and whiskers to learn about the world.

Hearing: Cats hear very high sounds that people may not hear, such as a mouse's squeak. Their ears can turn toward a sound and help them find it.

Low-light vision: A cat's eye has a special layer called the tapetum lucidum. It reflects light inside the eye. This helps cats see in very low light, but cats cannot see in complete darkness.

Whiskers: Whiskers give a cat touch information. They help the cat notice nearby objects, judge spaces, and keep its balance.

Check your understanding

Check: Cat Senses

1. What does the tapetum lucidum do?

Sample answer

It reflects light back through the eye a second time. This lets a cat see in near-darkness.

Reading

What Your Cat's Body Is Saying

Cat body language shows how a cat feels. Look at the ears, eyes, whiskers, tail, and whole body together.

Relaxed cat: The ears point forward. The eyes are soft or half closed. The whiskers rest near the face. The body is loose, and the tail may stand upright. This cat feels safe.

Curious cat: The ears and whiskers point forward. The eyes are open. The cat may move toward something new. Its tail may stand up.

Stressed cat: The ears turn sideways or lie flat. The eyes are wide. The body may crouch, and the cat may hide. Together, these signs are a stress signal that the cat needs quiet space.

Check your understanding

Check: Body Language

1. A cat's ears are flat and its tail is puffed up. Is this cat relaxed, curious, or stressed?

Sample answer

The cat is stressed. Flat ears and a puffed tail are clear stress signals. The best response is to give the cat quiet space.

Reading

Daily Care for a Happy Cat

Simple daily care helps a cat stay comfortable.

Fresh water: Give fresh, clean water at all times. Wash and refill the water bowl every day. A water fountain is optional; a clean bowl also works.

Safe interactive play: Give the cat interactive toys. A wand toy lets the cat follow a moving toy while your hands stay away from its claws.

Scratching: Cats need to scratch. Scratching removes the old outer cover from their claws. Give the cat a sturdy scratching post that does not wobble.

Clean litter: Put the litter box in a quiet place that the cat can reach. Scoop solid waste from it at least once a day.

A quiet sleep place: Give the cat a clean, dry place to rest. A soft, warm blanket or towel makes a simple bed. Wash the bedding often.

Veterinary care: A cat should see a veterinarian at least once a year, and at once if it is sick or hurt. A veterinarian can give advice for the cat's age and health.

Check your understanding

Check: Daily Care

1. Name two things you should do every day to care for a cat.

Sample answer

Any two of these are correct: give fresh water, play with the cat using a safe toy, clean the litter box, and make sure the cat has a quiet place to sleep.

What you can do now

Final Reflection

  1. You see a cat with wide eyes, forward whiskers, and a tall tail with a curled tip. What is the cat doing? What would you do next?

  2. A friend wants to get their first cat. What is the most important care habit you would tell them about, and why?

Vocabulary

Useful Topic Vocabulary

tapetum lucidum
a special layer in a cat's eye that reflects light and helps it see in the dark
whiskers
long, stiff hairs on a cat's face that sense air movement and help with balance
pupil
the black circle in the centre of the eye that controls how much light enters
rotate
to turn around a central point; cats rotate their ears to find sounds
stress signal
a body sign that shows an animal feels afraid or unsafe
scratching post
a tall object cats scratch to keep their claws healthy and stretch their muscles
litter box
a container filled with special material where an indoor cat goes to the toilet
vaccination
a medical treatment that protects a cat from certain diseases
