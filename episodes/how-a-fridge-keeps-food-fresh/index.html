Introduction

A fridge keeps food fresh by keeping it cold. Cold slows the growth of many bacteria that can make people sick. This lesson explains how a fridge keeps food fresh and safe, and what you can do at home.

Reading

Part 1: Cold Temperature Slows Bacteria

Some bacteria are tiny germs that can make people sick. Many bacteria grow quickly when food is warm.

Food-safety guides call 4°C to 60°C the Danger Zone. A fridge should keep food at 4°C or colder.

The cold does not kill all bacteria, but it slows their growth. This helps food stay safe longer.

Check: Cold Temperature

1. How does a cold fridge help keep food safe?

Sample answer

Reading

Part 2: The Right Temperature Setting

The FDA says a fridge should be at 4°C (40°F) or colder. A freezer should be at -18°C (0°F) or colder.

A fridge dial may not show the real temperature. Put a fridge thermometer inside and check it often. Do not pack the fridge too tightly, because cold air needs room to move.

Sample answer

Reading

Part 3: How to Store Food Correctly

Raw meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs can carry germs. The CDC says to keep these foods separate from other food.

Put raw meat or seafood in a sealed container so its juice cannot leak. Keep other foods in covered containers or sealed bags. Clean spills in the fridge quickly so germs do not spread.

Check: Storing Food Correctly

1. How can you stop raw meat juice from touching other food?

Sample answer

Keep the raw meat separate in a sealed container.

Reading

Part 4: Refrigerate Food Quickly

The CDC says to refrigerate food that can spoil within two hours. If the food is in heat above 32°C (90°F), refrigerate it within one hour.

Put a large amount of warm food into several clean, shallow containers. Small amounts cool faster. Then put them in the fridge promptly.
