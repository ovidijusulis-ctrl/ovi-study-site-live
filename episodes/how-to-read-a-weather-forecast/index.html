Introduction

Weather Forecasts

Reading a weather forecast to plan your day is a useful daily skill. A forecast can show temperature, clouds, wind, and rain chance. You can use those four clues to choose clothes, decide what to bring, and make a backup plan.

Reading

Temperature: Hot, Warm, Cool, or Cold

A forecast often shows a high temperature and a low temperature. The high is the warmest temperature expected.

The low is the coolest. First check whether the numbers use Celsius or Fahrenheit.

Imagine a high of 22°C and a low of 12°C. A light shirt may work at midday, but you may want a jacket later.

Check: Temperature

1. The high is 22°C and the low is 12°C. Why might you bring a jacket?

Sample answer

The day may feel warm at midday, but it can be much cooler later.

Reading

Sky Conditions: Sunny, Partly Cloudy, and Cloudy

Sky words describe how much of the sky has clouds. The National Weather Service uses sunny or clear for very few clouds.

Partly cloudy means some clouds and some open sky. Cloudy means clouds cover nearly all the sky. Clouds alone do not tell you the rain chance, so check the rain percent too.

Check: Sky Conditions

1. If a forecast says partly cloudy, what will the sky look like?

Sample answer

There will be some clouds and some open sky.

Reading

Wind: Light, Breezy, and Windy

A wind forecast gives a direction and a speed. The speed may be in miles per hour, or mph.

The National Weather Service calls 0–5 mph light wind, 15–25 mph breezy, and 20–30 mph windy. The ranges can overlap, so read the number too. On a breezy day, hold loose things and choose a jacket that closes.

Check: Wind

1. A forecast says breezy at 18 mph. Which information is most exact: the word or the number?

Sample answer

The number, 18 mph, is the most exact information.

Reading

Rain Chance: What Percent Means

A rain percent is the probability of precipitation, or POP. It is the chance of measurable rain at your location during the forecast period.

It is not the percent of the day that it will rain. The National Weather Service calls 20% a slight chance, 30–50% a chance, and 60–70% likely. Always check which time period the percent describes.

Reading a Full Forecast

Now imagine this forecast: high 22°C, low 12°C, partly cloudy, breezy at 18 mph, and 60% rain in the afternoon. Bring a light jacket for the cooler time.

Keep a hat secure in the wind. Take an umbrella and choose an indoor backup for the afternoon. One short forecast can help you make several useful choices.
