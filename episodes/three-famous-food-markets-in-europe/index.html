Reading

La Boqueria: Colour and Choice in Barcelona

La Boqueria stands on La Rambla, but its story began before the covered market. In 1217, records described meat stalls there.

Many early sellers were farmers from villages around Barcelona, bringing food into the city. Traders moved to the present site in 1836.

Today, more than 200 traders open their shutters. Visitors pass bright fruit and vegetables, olives and preserved foods, meat, fish and seafood.

Small bars serve food beside the shopping stalls. The market still connects two groups: people who sell food and people who come to shop, eat and talk. It has a long history, but it is also part of everyday city life.

Marché des Enfants Rouges: Old Paris, Many Flavours

Marché des Enfants Rouges is Paris's oldest covered food market. Its unusual name means “Market of the Red Children.”

It remembers children from a nearby home who wore red clothes. In the 1990s, there was a plan to replace the old market with a car park.

Traders and neighbours protested and even slept outside to save it. Today, their market is full of different people and foods.

Tom's team serves Moroccan chicken skewers and couscous. Julienne cooks Creole food with flavours from Cameroon. Visitors can also buy farm cheese, Breton buckwheat galettes, oysters, fresh fruit juice, and organic fruit and vegetables.

Borough Market: Produce and Street Food in London

Borough Market's story goes back about 1,000 years, when people sold food near London Bridge. In 1756, local parishioners moved it to the place where it stands today.

The market is now a charity. Volunteer trustees run it for shoppers, traders and neighbours, so it is still a community place, not only a tourist sight.

More than 100 businesses sell food there. Applebee's offers British seafood, Arabica serves Middle Eastern meze, falafel and shawarma, and Bao Borough makes bao buns. Visitors can shop for cheese and other ingredients, choose a hot lunch, and meet some of the people who feed London today.

Tipping in Spain, France and the UK

Tipping customs change between Spain, France and the UK. At a simple market stall or self-service counter, people normally pay the displayed price unless the seller says otherwise.

In Spain, a tip is optional, and some people simply round up the bill. In France, restaurant prices normally include service, so extra money is optional.

In the UK, a restaurant bill may include a discretionary or compulsory service charge. A visitor should read the bill before adding more money.

The rules can feel different, but you do not need to guess. The safest habit in all three countries is simple: check the bill, then ask.

Roleplay

Roleplay 1: Shop at La Boqueria

Seller

Hello. What can I get you?

You

What is popular here?

Seller

These green olives are very popular.

You

Is this local?

Seller

Yes, these olives are from Catalonia.

You

Great. Two hundred grams, please, and two peaches.

Seller

Of course. Anything else?

You

No, thank you. Do you take cards?

Seller

Yes. That's six euros, please.

You

Here you are.

Seller

Thank you. Enjoy your food.

Scene 2: Check Ingredients in Paris

You are at a prepared-food counter inside Marché des Enfants Rouges, Paris's oldest covered food market. Around you, cooks serve dishes from several cultures. A rice-and-vegetable dish looks good, but you have a nut allergy. First ask, “What is in this?” Then ask, “Does this contain nuts?” and say, “I am allergic to nuts.” If the dish is unsafe, point to a sealed bottle of water and say, “One of these, please.”

Your goal is to check the dish, explain your allergy, refuse food that is not safe for you, and order a drink instead.

Roleplay 2: Ask About a Nut Allergy

Cook

Hello. Would you like this rice-and-vegetable dish?

You

It looks good. What is in this?

Cook

It has rice, vegetables and a sauce.

You

Does this contain nuts? I am allergic to nuts.

Cook

Yes. The sauce contains almonds, and other food here may also touch nuts.

You

Thank you for telling me. I won't have the dish.

Cook

Of course. Would you like a drink?

You

Yes. One of these, please.

Cook

Here you are.

You

Thank you.

Roleplay 3: Order and Pay in London

Counter Worker

Hello. What can I get you?

You

One cheese toastie, please.

Counter Worker

That's seven pounds.

You

Do you take cards?

Counter Worker

Yes. You can tap here.

You

Is there a service charge?

Counter Worker

No. The price is seven pounds.

You

Do I need to leave a tip?

Counter Worker

No, not at this counter.

You

Great. I'll pay by card.

Counter Worker

Thank you. Enjoy your lunch.

    Need a starting line?

    Start with: What is in this?
